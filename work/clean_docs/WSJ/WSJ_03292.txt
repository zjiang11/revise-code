With Google, there is a certain value to clarity.
It is a point remembering each year when the company trots out offerings that appear to have little to do with running a $70 billion-a-year advertising business. Tuesday was such an occasion. Google kicked off its annual I/O developers' conference by showing off a virtual reality platform called Daydream and a voice-controlled speaker similar to Amazon's Echo. It has even designed chips to power artificial intelligence. After two hours, not a word had been spoken about click-through rates and other key metrics of the advertising business.
For investors, the event can be a letdown. Often misunderstood is how efforts to advance space-age sounding technology can underpin Google's value proposition of keeping Internet users engaged with its services.
That is crucial. The Android mobile operating system gives Google a powerful position as desktop action moves to mobile. But the future will also include users going online through voice, or in virtual reality. Google needs to be in all those places to stay competitive -- and generate new business.
So it needs far-out efforts, even if they cost. The segment called "other bets" ran up an operating loss of $3.7 billion for the 12-month period ended March 31.Even so, core Google earned $24.5 billion in operating profit for the same period while still growing revenue by double digits. Meanwhile, Alphabet shares fetch less than 18 times forward earnings, excluding net cash.
That isn't a high price to pay for sky-high ambitions.
