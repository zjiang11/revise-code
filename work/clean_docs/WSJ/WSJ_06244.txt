News Corp swung to a profit in the fourth quarter, boosted by growth in its real- estate and book publishing segments.
The New York-based company, which owns The Wall Street Journal and HarperCollins Publishers, said revenue increased 6% to $2.58 billion in the quarter ended June 30, better than analysts were expecting.
Most of the company's segments posted revenue increases, except for its news media unit, which owns news organizations in the U.K. and Australia as well as the New York Post.
Growth was driven largely by higher revenue at REA Group in its digital real estate unit, higher physical and digital book sales and growth in the professional information business at the Dow Jones segment, the company said.
The company continues to review its portfolio, Chief Executive Robert Thomson said. As part of that review, it is evaluating its options for Australian video business Foxtel Group after receiving interest from a potential suitor.
The company's partnership with generative AI startup OpenAI is expected to be lucrative, Thomson said.
News Corp's Class A shares rose more than 3% in after-hours trading.
Dow Jones, the publisher of the Journal, Barron's and MarketWatch, reported revenue growth of 4% to $566 million and segment earnings of $137 million.
The Journal averaged 3.8 million digital subscriptions in the quarter, an increase of 73,000 from the previous quarter. Including the print edition, total subscriptions to the Journal averaged 4.3 million subscriptions.
Book Publishing revenue grew by 15% to $512 million, while segment earnings were $57 million. Top titles for HarperCollins included the "Bridgerton" series by Julia Quinn," The Midnight Feast" by Lucy Foley and "When the Moon Hatched" by Sarah A. Parker.
