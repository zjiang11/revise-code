CIOs are becoming more revenue-driven, a new report shows.
As information technology becomes more central to many businesses, 63% of chief executive officers are focusing their CIOs on IT projects that make money, according to a survey of 3,352 CIOs and technology leaders conducted by global professional recruiting firm and IT outsourcing provider Harvey Nash and KPMG LLC. That compares to 37% of CEOs who want their CIOs to prioritize projects that save money.
Similarly, boards have placed less emphasis on traditional operational priorities. Since 2013, there has been a 30% drop in boards asking CIOs to focus on saving money.
During that same period, CIOs say there has been a 27% decrease in boards asking them to focus on delivering consistent and stable IT performance to the business, according to the survey.
The job requirements that have either grown or stayed relatively stable over the last four years are all outward facing, according to the report. Those include better engagement with customers, revenue growth and improving business processes.
For example, in 2014, Intel CIO Kim Stevenson implemented advanced analytics software that generated $351 million in revenue for the semiconductor maker. One of the first analytics projects was used to help salespeople become more efficient in outbound calls to resellers. The system created a probability model, based on machine learning, that described what a sales win looked like. It told the salespeople which resellers to call, and in which order. Similarly, GE CIO Jim Fowler used analytics to help generate revenue when he was at GE Capital. He used automated tools to drive $400 million into GE Capital over two years, CIO Journal reported in March 2015. That revenue came through a number of IT projects including a fleet analytics tool and platform called Touchless that underwrote $200 million of volume coming into the business.
