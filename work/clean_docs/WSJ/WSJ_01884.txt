Sony Corp. plans to keep making smartphones -- while weaning its customers off them.
"People have become too obsessed with smartphones," said Hiroki Totoki, chief executive of the electronics maker's mobile unit, in an interview.
When Mr. Totoki took charge of Sony Mobile Communications Inc. in late 2014, he said his first job was to stanch the struggling unit's losses. With restructuring nearly complete, he says he now wants to redefine how people communicate.
That might seem like a tall order for Sony, whose share of the global smartphone market is just 2%, according to research firm IHS. But growth in smartphones is slowing for other makers, too, and Sony says it is looking to a future in which other devices occupy the cutting edge of mobile technology.
At the Mobile World Congress in Barcelona this week, Sony introduced Xperia Ear, a smartphone-companion earpiece that can read out emails, map directions or other notifications. The aim is to free users of the need to continually check their phones, Mr. Totoki said. Sony hasn't announced the price of the gadget, which is compatible with Android phones.
Sony says Xperia Ear will be available in major markets starting this summer, though Izumi Kawanishi, an executive vice president of Sony Mobile, described it as an experimental product. He said the company is also working on other gadgets, incorporating technologies such as augmented reality and machine learning, as it seeks to improve communications not just among people but between individuals and their devices. While many electronics companies have rolled out so-called wearable technologies, including smartwatches, smart glasses and even smart shoes, there have been few successes. Mr. Totoki said Sony hopes to create a new market with its devices. Otherwise, he said, smartphone companies, including Sony's mobile unit, may struggle to survive.
Sony has pruned its mobile business by cutting back in the U.S. and Chinese markets.
