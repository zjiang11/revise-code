[Financial Analysis and Commentary]
More than two billion people now use Google's Android mobile operating system, which is remarkable considering how long it takes for the company's latest innovations to get into most of their hands.
Many of them were on display Wednesday as Google kicked off its annual I/O developers conference. Under parent company Alphabet, Google has invested billions to inject artificial intelligence into its global network.
But while Android powered 85% of smartphones sold last year, according to IDC, its updates take their time to spread. The most recent went into wide release last August but currently runs just 7% of Android devices.
The company is trying to speed up. A new effort called Project Treble will provide more of the basic software that chip makers and handset designers need to update devices. That may help more users take advantage earlier of the latest updates.
A less fragmented market could help Google boost the return from its Android investment. The segment containing Google's mobile app and content store generated $11.1 billion in revenue for the 12-month period ended March 31, up 47% year over year. But that segment also contains other business and it still trailed Apple's $26.5 billion in service revenue for the same period.
Keeping more Android users current should boost Google's upsell abilities and generate more effective advertising, better serving the company's prime moneymaker. That would be a sweet deal for investors.
