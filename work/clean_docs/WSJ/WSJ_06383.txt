Business-software provider Salesforce reduced its outlook for subscription and support revenue and issued a downbeat forecast for the current quarter.
The company said there was cooling demand from customers who were taking longer to place orders for its software services, despite excitement around Salesforce's artificial-intelligence products.
"We saw elongated deal cycles, deal compression and high levels of budget scrutiny," said Brian Millham, president of Salesforce, on a conference call.
The stock fell 16% in after-hours trading. Before the earnings, Salesforce shares had risen 3.2% since the start of the year.
Chief Executive Marc Benioff expressed enthusiasm that the company's AI products would soon become a major tailwind to sales as more businesses signed up to use their new tools, which use AI to improve marketing emails and business intelligence.
The business software provider said it now expects subscription and support revenue, the lion's share of the company's business, to grow just below 10% in the current fiscal year. The company had previously forecast growth of about 10% or above 10% in constant currency.
Salesforce also reduced its full-year guidance for operating margin.
The company expects revenue to be up 7% to 8% in the current quarter to $9.2 billion to $9.25 billion. Analysts polled by FactSet are expecting revenue of $9.35 billion for the period.
