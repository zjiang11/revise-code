LinkedIn Corp.'s growth unexpectedly accelerated in the second quarter, as the professional social network announced a new push to target sales teams, signaling that it isn't satisfied with its reliance on corporate recruiters.
LinkedIn unveiled a revamped version of its Sales Navigator software, which it will offer as a stand-alone, subscription service. Before Thursday, the software was part of LinkedIn's main offering and catered mostly to individuals. The company says the new product will scour LinkedIn's database of 313 million users to offer salespeople suggestions on potential customers, and then help them find mutual acquaintances to get introductions.
For the second quarter, LinkedIn said revenue rose 47%, to $534 million from $363.7 million a year earlier. That was slightly faster growth than in the first quarter, when LinkedIn's revenue climbed 46%.
But it swung to a $1 million loss, partly because of its February acquisition of Bright, a company that uses data to connect job prospects with employers. In the year-earlier period, LinkedIn posted a profit of $3.7 million.
Excluding certain expenses, LinkedIn said it would have earned 51 cents a share, up from 38 cents a year earlier. Analysts had expected earnings of 39 cents a share on that basis.
LinkedIn said its membership ranks increased to 313 million from 296 million in the first quarter. Unique visitors to LinkedIn increased slightly, but page views declined to 25.4 billion from 26.9 billion in the first quarter.
LinkedIn shares rose 7.5% in after-hours trading, after falling 3.6%, to $180.64 in 4 p.m. trading on the New York Stock Exchange. LinkedIn's shares have fallen 13% over the past year.
LinkedIn said mobile devices now account for 45% of its traffic, up from 43% last quarter.
LinkedIn's growth was propelled by its mainstay Talent Solutions unit, which sells to corporate recruiters and human-resources staffers. Revenue in that unit grew 49%, to $322 million, and accounted for 60% of the company's revenue. Advertising revenue grew 44% to $106 million, and revenue from premium subscriptions increased 44% to $105 million.
Arvind Bhatia, an analyst for Sterne Agee, said the results were among LinkedIn's best in the past year and a half. He said LinkedIn's expansion into China earlier this year is a potentially lucrative area of long-term growth and the new sales product is a good "medium term" growth engine.
With its push deeper into sales, LinkedIn is hoping to tap a large market and boost its growth. LinkedIn had been hinting that it would put more effort into its sales software business. Chief Executive Jeff Weiner told investors in May that the company was investing in research and development in the area, and hyped a new lead generation tool.
J.P. Morgan analyst Doug Anmuth told clients July 10 that he was "encouraged" by the new product, which he expected to have more functionality than prior versions and could be sold more to companies, instead of individuals.
LinkedIn's bigger move into sales could pit it against the industry's largest player, Salesforce.com Inc., says Vik Singh, CEO of Infer, maker of a software program that uses machine learning to predict successful sales leads.
