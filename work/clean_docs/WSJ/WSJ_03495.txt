[Financial Analysis and Commentary]
Facebook is a massive and growing company. But one thing hasn't kept pace: its profits. The social-media giant beat expectations for third-quarter earnings Wednesday while revenue rose 41%. Monthly active users of 1.55 billion grew 14% from the previous year.
Facebook's investment in its advertising platform appears to have paid off. Its advertising-revenue growth of 45% over the previous year was two percentage points higher than the second quarter. That was the first acceleration since the first quarter of 2014. Mobile-advertising revenue growth of 72% easily outpaced the second quarter's 61% rate.
But while Facebook's revenue growth is striking for a huge company, growth has come at a cost. Research-and-development expenses have ballooned to 28% of revenue from 19% in 2014's third quarter, much of which is due to share-based compensation for engineers.
That matters because a good chunk of these expenses don't make it into Facebook's pro forma earnings. The company's pro forma income from operations was $6.48 billion in the first nine months of 2015, up 30% from a year ago. But reported operating income actually fell 5% to $3.67 billion.
And dilution from share issuance means revenue growth is spread over a bigger shareholder pie. So while revenue grew 41%, revenue per share rose only 24%.
Granted, R&D investment is essential to help Facebook grow into its lofty multiple of 62 times 2016 earnings estimates. Only some of that spending goes to its core advertising business, though.
Much also goes to projects such as artificial intelligence, virtual reality and bringing Internet service to emerging markets. It will be a long time before some of these "moonshot" investments pay off -- if they do.
For investors, Facebook's spending comes with increasing risk.
