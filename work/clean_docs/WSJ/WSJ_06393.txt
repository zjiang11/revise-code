Technology stocks led the Nasdaq to new highs as investors continued to bet on the strength of U.S. consumers and interest-rate cuts.
The tech-laden Nasdaq Composite rose 0.7% to 16794.87, eclipsing the record it set Wednesday after a Labor Department report showed inflation easing in April. The S&P 500, which traded at record levels before a late-session pullback, added less than 0.1%. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 0.5%, or 197 points, from Friday's record close.
Norwegian Cruise Line Holdings added 7.6% to lead the S&P 500 higher after it raised this year's profit expectations and executives gave an upbeat presentation to investors at the New York Stock Exchange. Rivals Royal Caribbean and Carnival gained 4.1% and 7.3%, respectively.
Investors will get a closer look at consumer spending Tuesday, when Lowe's, Macy's, Autozone and Urban Outfitters are scheduled to report quarterly results.
On Wednesday, investors will get a fresh read on existing home sales and pore over minutes from the meeting three weeks ago of the Federal Open Market Committee for clues about the central bank's next move for interest rates.
The yield on benchmark 10-year Treasury notes rose to 4.436% from 4.419% on Friday.
Nvidia, the microchip maker at the center of the artificial-intelligence boom, is scheduled to report its latest results Wednesday afternoon. The shares rose 2.5% on Monday and are up 91% so far this year. Netflix added 3.2% to close at its highest price since late 2021.
Insurer Chubb was among the S&P 500's biggest losers, shedding 3.7%. The stock rose 8.4% to a record late last week after Warren Buffett's Berkshire Hathaway disclosed a big stake in the firm.
Natural-gas producers continued to heat up. EQT shares added 1.8% to bring their May climb to more than 14%. Comstock Resources, controlled by Dallas Cowboys owner Jerry Jones, is up 19% this month, including a 5.1% gain Monday. Warmer weather and rising electricity demand have lifted natural-gas futures 75% since late March, when they traded at some of the lowest prices on record.
Wheat futures rose to their highest price since last summer amid worry about the Russian crop. Soft-red winter wheat futures rose 5.6% in Chicago.
Overseas, the Stoxx Europe 600 rose 0.2%. At midday Tuesday, Japan's Nikkei 225 was up 0.2%, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng Index was down over 2%. S&P 500 futures were unchanged.
