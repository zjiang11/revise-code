Nearly all of the Magnificent Seven tech stocks fell Tuesday, dragging major indexes down.
Chip maker Nvidia -- the biggest winner of the market's artificial-intelligence mania -- was the only member of the group to post a gain.
In 2023, the group made up of Alphabet, Amazon.com, Apple, Meta Platforms, Microsoft, Nvidia and Tesla powered the market, helping to drive the S&P 500 up 24%. Stocks have continued to march higher this year, with the broad U.S. stock index and tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite closing at records Friday.
But trading has taken on a cautious tone so far this week ahead of key economic data, including the monthly jobs report Friday. Major indexes retreated Monday and extended their declines Tuesday.
The S&P 500 fell 1%, while the Nasdaq Composite lost 1.7%. The Dow Jones Industrial Average slid 1%, or about 405 points.
Within the Magnificent Seven, Tesla led the way lower Tuesday, sinking 3.9%. Microsoft, the largest U.S. stock by market value, lost 3%. Apple slipped 2.8%, retreating for a fifth consecutive trading day after new data showed that iPhone sales in China fell to start the year.
The synchronized gains by the seven stocks in 2023 have broken down in the new year. Nvidia shares are up 74% and Meta Platforms shares are up 38% in 2024, while Apple shares have slumped 12% and Tesla shares have tumbled 27%.
"There is some concern that things have run too quickly, too far, especially in 2023's Mag Seven," said Louise Goudy Willmering, a partner at wealth-management firm Crewe Advisors. "We're starting to see quite a bit of dispersion between the best performers and the worst performers in that group."
The stock market has managed to advance even as some of its tech heavyweights falter. The S&P 500 has risen 6.5% in 2024, notching 15 record closes along the way. The Nasdaq Composite is up 6.2% and last week logged its first record close since November 2021.
Nine of the S&P 500's 11 sectors are in positive territory in 2024. The technology group leads the gains and has advanced 10%, while the financial segment has added 7.1%, the healthcare group has risen 6.1% and the industrials group has gained 5.9%.
"You can see that the markets are actually broadening out," said Aaron Clark, portfolio manager at GW&K Investment Management.
Among individual stocks Tuesday, shares of Target jumped 12% after the retailer reported stronger-than-expected profit for the holiday quarter. Shares of GitLab plunged 21% after the software-development platform gave disappointing guidance for the year.
The yield on the benchmark 10-year Treasury note slipped to 4.136% from 4.218% on Monday. Yields fall as bond prices rise.
Gold jumped to a record of $2,133.50 a troy ounce. Its price has risen for four consecutive sessions.
Bitcoin rose to a record for the first time since late 2021, briefly crossing $69,000 Tuesday morning before falling below that level. Analysts have pointed to robust flows into the new exchange-traded funds holding bitcoin as a catalyst.
Overseas, the Stoxx Europe 600 slipped 0.2%. At midday Wednesday, Hong Kong's Hang Seng Index was up 0.9% after falling 2.6% Tuesday. Japan's Nikkei 225 was down 0.3% at midday Wednesday. S&P 500 futures were up 0.1%
