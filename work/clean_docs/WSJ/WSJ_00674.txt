The unknowable future is becoming just a bit more knowable.
It's the next stage in the evolution of information technology, beyond the early systems of record and the systems of engagement that allowed companies to interact with customers and employees in new ways, according to Richard Sherlund, chairman of software banking at Barclays PLC. "Now, we're going to systems of prediction.. . .I think this delivers a lot more value to the market," he said at the annual Wall Street Journal CIO Network meeting in San Francisco.
This latest generation of machine learning and artificial intelligence can reason in new ways. Neil Jacobstein, chair of artificial intelligence and robotics at Singularity University, said an AI recently beat some of the top poker players in the world. "This is a big deal," he said, because it signals the ability of AI to deal with incomplete information, bluffing and misinformation -- skills that businesses and public officials could use in any number of ways.
