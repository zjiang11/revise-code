Shares of nuclear-power producers fell after regulators dealt a blow to an Amazon.com effort to use more nuclear energy, as technology giants turn to the power source to fuel the development of artificial intelligence.
The Federal Energy Regulatory Commission late Friday blocked a request by Amazon and Talen Energy to siphon more electricity from a Pennsylvania nuclear plant to one of the internet retail company's data centers. Talen shares fell 2.2% on Monday.
Talen said it believed FERC had erred, and that the company was evaluating options.
The news hit other nuclear-heavy power companies, whose stocks have surged as Amazon, Alphabet's Google, Microsoft and other tech companies have inked deals to use carbon-free nuclear power to meet increasing AI-fueled demand for electricity.
Shares of Constellation Energy, which plans to restart Pennsylvania's Three Mile Island power plant to sell electricity to Microsoft, tumbled 12.5%. Shares of Vistra, Centrus Energy, NuScale Power and Public Service Enterprise Group also were hit Monday.
Despite the stock reaction, analysts said the ruling was a minor setback in the industry's push to capitalize on the energy source.
Talen can still restructure its expanded deal with Amazon, according to Oppenheimer analysts, and can already supply some power under the existing agreement.
FERC's rejection appeared to be more about technical issues than broader policy, UBS analysts said in a research note.
