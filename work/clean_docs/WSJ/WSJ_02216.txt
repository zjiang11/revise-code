International Business Machines posted higher revenue in the third quarter as demand for artificial intelligence offset a shaky consulting environment.
The tech company's generative AI book of business has now topped $3 billion, up more than $1 billion compared with the second quarter. About 20% of that is related to software solutions, with the rest coming from consulting.
"We're pretty confident around the growth trajectory," Chief Financial Officer James Kavanaugh said of the AI business.
Software and AI-related gains were offset in the quarter by flat consulting revenue and lower sales across IBM's infrastructure unit.
The consulting market has been challenged by customers "dynamically reprioritizing budgets and cutting back on short-term discretionary-based projects," Kavanaugh said.
He added that he believes IBM's position as an early-adopter in AI-based consulting will help the company in the long term.
Infrastructure sales, which fell 7%, will likely persist in the coming quarters, as the company is at the tail end of a three-year product cycle. The company's next-generation of hardware will be announced in the first half of 2025, and is later expected to provide a boost to earnings, Kavanaugh said.
Revenue in the latest quarter rose 1.5% to $14.97 billion but came in below the $15.08 billion that Wall Street analysts expected, according to FactSet.
IBM swung to a net loss of $330 million, compared with a profit of $1.7 billion last year, hurt by a one-time charge of about $2.7 billion related to the transfer of a portion of its U.S. defined-benefit pension plan obligations to a third-party insurer.
Adjusted per-share earnings were $2.30, beating the $2.22 that analysts surveyed by FactSet expected.
IBM expects fourth-quarter revenue growth to be similar to the third quarter on a constant-currency basis.
