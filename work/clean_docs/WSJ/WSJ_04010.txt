The S&P 500 crossed 5000 for the first time in intraday trading, the latest milestone for a U.S. stock market powered by a resilient economy and subsiding inflation.
The broad U.S. stock index popped over 5000 in the final minute of trading Thursday, according to Dow Jones Market Data, before settling slightly below the mark. The S&P 500's daily advance of 0.1% to 4997.91 was enough for another record close, its ninth of 2024.
The benchmark index is up 4.8% in 2024, helped by gains in shares of companies ranging from technology to healthcare to financials.
The Dow Jones Industrial Average gained 0.1%, or about 49 points, to its 11th record close of 2024. The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite rallied 0.2%, leaving it 1.6% off its record close from November 2021.
A drumbeat of earnings reports has driven moves in individual stocks, including Walt Disney on Thursday, and continued to shape the outlook for the broad market.
Analysts expect that profits from companies in the S&P 500 grew by 2.8% in the fourth quarter from a year earlier, according to FactSet. For 2024, they anticipate earnings will grow by 11%.
The rosy outlook for profits has helped fuel the advance for stocks.
"Over history, stock prices follow earnings growth," said Mike Mullaney, director of global markets research at Boston Partners. "A lot of people have that ingrained in their psyche."
Investors have embraced signs that the economy remains strong despite the Federal Reserve's campaign of interest-rate increases. The latest jobs report showed employers added 353,000 jobs in January, nearly double what economists surveyed by The Wall Street Journal expected.
Inflation has cooled from historic highs, meanwhile, leading central-bank officials to consider when they will begin lowering interest rates.
The stock market's advance has left it looking more expensive than it has in some time. The S&P 500 traded Wednesday at 20.38 times its projected earnings over the next 12 months, its highest since February 2022 and above a 10-year average of 17.95, according to FactSet.
Traders are betting that the Fed will cut rates this year by more than central-bank officials are projecting, raising the potential for disappointment.
And in bond markets, benchmark yields have risen lately. That can weigh on stocks by raising borrowing costs for companies and consumers, as well as by increasing the appeal of lower-risk investments.
The yield on the 10-year U.S. Treasury note rose to 4.169% from 4.108% on Wednesday.
"I think the S&P 500 rally kind of peters out at this point," said Saira Malik, chief investment officer at Nuveen.
Earnings season drove moves in individual stocks. Walt Disney shares jumped 11% after the entertainment company beat earnings estimates and showed momentum in its streaming business.
Shares of Arm surged 48% after the designer of mobile-phone circuitry gave a better-than-expected outlook. The company is making inroads in chips for data centers where artificial-intelligence computations are performed.
Hershey shares rose 4.1% after the maker of Reese's peanut-butter cups projected sales would continue to grow this year. PayPal shares dropped 11% after an underwhelming outlook.
Benchmark Brent crude gaining 3.1% to $81.63 a barrel.
Overseas, the Stoxx Europe 600 index slipped 0.1%. At midday Friday, Japan's Nikkei 225 was up 0.45%. In much of Asia, Friday marks the beginning of the Lunar New Year and many markets are closed. S&P 500 futures fell 0.1%.
