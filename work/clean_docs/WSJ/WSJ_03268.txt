Ford Motor Co. and Lyft Inc. said Wednesday they would develop self-driving vehicles for the ride-hailing service, adding to a growing number of alliances between auto makers and tech companies jockeying for control of the road.
The two companies plan to design software that lets Ford vehicles communicate with Lyft's app-based services. Lyft, based in San Francisco, is playing catch-up in some respects to larger crosstown rival Uber Technologies Inc., which has been testing robot cars in Pittsburgh, Tempe, Ariz., and parts of California.
Lyft has struck a series of partnerships with companies developing the technology, including Waymo, the self-driving car unit of Google parent Alphabet Inc., Tata Motors Ltd.'s Jaguar Land Rover and startups Drive.ai and NuTonomy Inc. And Lyft is forming its own autonomous-car development division.
Lyft also has a partnership with Ford rival General Motors Co., which invested about $500 million in the company and added a board member. Last year, GM said it planned to work with Lyft to put self-driving taxis on the road sometime this year. A Lyft spokeswoman said the GM agreement wasn't exclusive.
Last month, new Ford Chief Executive Jim Hackett said his company was evaluating how it could deploy fully autonomous vehicles within the next four years. The Dearborn, Mich., company envisions vehicles with no steering wheels or pedals and no need for a human driver. And it plans to invest $1 billion over the next five years in Argo AI, an artificial-intelligence company in which it acquired a majority stake.
Lyft, meanwhile, is considering accepting a roughly $1 billion investment from Alphabet, people familiar with the matter have said. The possibility points to the complicated web of relationships in ride hailing, as Google is also an investor in Uber.
