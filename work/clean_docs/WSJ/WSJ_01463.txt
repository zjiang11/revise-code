Intel Corp. said it would acquire Movidius, a startup focusing on computer vision technology, in the Silicon Valley giant's latest bet on specialized chips for artificial-intelligence applications.
Terms of the transaction weren't disclosed. The deal, subject to certain closing conditions, was announced in blog posts on Monday.
Movidius, founded nearly 11 years ago in Ireland, raised over $85 million from venture-capital firms. It has developed algorithms and chips it calls vision-processing units for use in equipment including drones, robots and augmented- and virtual-reality devices.
The company was an early partner with Alphabet Inc.'s Google unit in Project Tango, an effort designed to build technology into smartphones to measure and map surrounding objects and interior spaces.
Intel is seeking to move into new markets beyond the shrinking personal computer business, where sales of microprocessors are its largest source of revenue. The company has announced a series of moves to provide technology for drones, wearable devices and other connected gadgets associated with a trend called the Internet of Things.
Intel's offerings include RealSense, a combination of chips and 3-D camera technology that it plans to incorporate into a prototype virtual-reality headset called Project Alloy. Intel and Movidius said combining their efforts should spur significant advances in vision-related fields.
"We see massive potential for Movidius to accelerate our initiatives in new and emerging technologies," wrote Josh Walden, senior vice president and general manager of Intel's new technology group.
