International Business Machines Corp. and ServiceNow Inc. are partnering on an artificial-intelligence-based offering aimed at helping organizations predict, prevent and remediate outages and other information-technology problems.
The offering will combine ServiceNow's IT Service Management and IT Operations Management systems with IBM's recently introduced Watson AIOps.
Watson AIOps uses AI to automate how companies detect and respond to IT anomalies. The AIOps platform also can recommend how to fix problems based on an analysis of how past events were resolved. The new offering also could launch an automated ServiceNow process to fix a problem.
The combination of the two services could help companies find problems about 60% faster than human monitors, IBM said.
Without an automated solution, it often takes a dedicated team to spot irregular IT activity. But IBM has millions of scenarios built into its Watson AIOps to help IT organizations remedy any problem, said IBM Chief Executive Arvind Krishna.
"We are continuously improving what we're doing there," said Mr. Krishna, "and then being able to put all that knowledge right back in the client solution."
The complexity of IT operations in recent years has exploded, and finding a problem across an IT environment that includes hardware, networks and applications on-premise and in the cloud is difficult, said Stephen Elliot, a vice president at research group International Data Corp.
Not catching a problem can be expensive. Unplanned system downtime can cost a large company hundreds of thousands of dollars an hour in lost revenue, not to mention damage to its reputation, Mr. Elliot said.
