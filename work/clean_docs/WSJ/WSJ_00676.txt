BEIJING -- A mysterious character named "Master" has swept through China, defeating many of the world's top players in the ancient strategy game of Go.
Master played online with inhuman speed, barely pausing to think. With a wide-eyed cartoon fox as an avatar, Master made moves that seemed foolish but inevitably led to victory this week over the world's reigning Go champion, Ke Jie of China.
Master revealed itself Wednesday as an updated version of AlphaGo, an artificial-intelligence program designed by the DeepMind unit of Alphabet Inc.'s Google.
AlphaGo made history in March by beating South Korea's top Go player in four of five games in Seoul. Now, it has defeated the world champion. It was dramatic theater and the latest sign that artificial intelligence is peerless in solving complex but defined problems.
Master's arrival has shaken China's human Go players, who say it upended thousands of years of the game's strategic wisdom.
"After humanity spent thousands of years improving our tactics, computers tell us that humans are completely wrong," Mr. Ke, 19 years old, wrote on Chinese social-media platform Weibo after his defeat.
Go was invented about 2,500 years ago in China and remains popular across East Asia. Players take turns placing black and white stones on a grid, seeking to capture patches of territory by surrounding them.
