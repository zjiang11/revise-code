[Financial Analysis and Commentary]
Big parties tend to run up big bills. For many chip stocks, that bill is coming due.
Semiconductors have been the best-performing sector in tech this year by far. At its peak last week, the PHLX Semiconductor Index was up more than 34% for the year to a 16-year high. The group even managed a strong postelection boost. By the close of November, the index was up 6% since the vote, outperforming even the Dow industrials.
But nothing lasts forever. A harsh selloff Thursday clipped much of the recent gains. Citigroup noted that investors are rotating out of chips and into financials and energy. And while the group was faring better Friday, investors should expect more volatility ahead.
Still, before the next rush of sell calls, consider that the chip sector isn't exactly overvalued. Strong earnings expansion has kept valuation in check. The PHLX is averaging about 14.6 times forward earnings. And some of the most damaged names are still in strong positions. Broadcom, Qorvo and Skyworks registered declines last week, mostly on reports of Apple trimming iPhone production. But that is a short-term problem, as future smartphones are likely to make further use of the types of radio-frequency chips these companies make.
Nvidia, whose shares trade above 30 times forward earnings, seems an easy target to take some profits. But the company has taken up a strong position in automotive and deep learning -- two major growth segments. That sort of promise should encourage investors to keep some chips in the game.
