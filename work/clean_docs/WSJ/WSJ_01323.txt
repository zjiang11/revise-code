[Financial Analysis and Commentary]
Adobe calling off its $20 billion acquisition of Figma might be the least surprising news of the day. But it raises the stakes for the software maker's bet on generative artificial intelligence, which could take longer to pay off than some expect.
Wall Street had grown increasingly skeptical that the deal would go through as regulatory pressure grew. Hence, Monday's announcement that the two companies would go their separate ways didn't alarm investors; Adobe's shares rose 2.5% in Monday's trading.
"If [the] deal does not close, we would expect Adobe to turn aggressive on share buybacks," wrote Brent Thill of Jefferies in a note to clients ahead of Adobe's fiscal fourth-quarter results last week. About $10 billion of the acquisition price was expected to be paid in cash, though Adobe will now pay Figma a $1 billion termination fee.
The bigger question facing Adobe is when its new AI-based services, such as Firefly, will start generating real growth. Last week's report disappointed investors after Adobe projected revenue growth of about 10% for the new fiscal year ending next November, which is flat with the growth seen in the company's just-ended year.
Adobe's share price fell more than 6% following the report, though the stock is still up 75% for the year -- and it stands at more than double its level following a sharp selloff that was sparked by the announcement of the Figma deal in September 2022.
Jake Roberge of William Blair wrote Monday morning that "we believe the advent of generative AI has significantly changed Adobe's market opportunity since the announcement of the Figma acquisition, which should give investors more comfort in Adobe's long-term growth profile."
But Tyler Radke of Citigroup noted that "long-term competitive issues may persist," as Figma, Canva and other Adobe competitors look to harness AI for their own offerings.
