Facebook is going back in time.
The social network said Thursday it is giving users the option to view a more traditional friends and family feed, similar to what it offered when it launched nearly two decades ago.
The feature, called Feeds, will allow users to see posts from friends, family and pages they follow with the most recent content first, Meta Platforms Inc. Chief Executive Mark Zuckerberg said.
This differs from the algorithm-driven content that appears in Facebook's main feed, which Meta has said would rely more on artificial intelligence to recommend content to users. That would make Facebook more like TikTok's For You feed, where users don't see content based on accounts they follow but instead from what TikTok's algorithm determines they are interested in.
"One of the most requested features for Facebook is to make sure people don't miss friends' posts," Mr. Zuckerberg said in a Facebook post. He added that the platform's home tab will still be a personalized feed with recommended content from Facebook's discovery engine, which shows posts that it thinks users would like to see.
"The Feeds tab will give you a way to customize and control your experience further," he said.
Facebook users have clamored for years for better control over what they see in their feeds. Other social media platforms have given users the option to view content in chronological order. Twitter Inc. said a few years ago that users could switch from latest and top tweets in their timeline. Instagram, which is owned by Meta, made a similar announcement earlier this year, giving users more options to choose what they see in their feed.
Facebook users have been able to manually make their feeds show them most-recent content by changing their settings, but they would be bumped out of that feed if they closed the website or app.
