[Financial Analysis and Commentary]
Nvidia is playing a high-stakes game these days. Fortunately, it continues to play its hand well.
The chip maker has become closely tied to the cloud, most specifically to the sizable flow of capital spending by major cloud-service operators that are buying up the company's graphics processors that are specially designed to enable artificial intelligence in data centers. It's still a relatively small business for Nvidia, but it's expanding fast. Nvidia's sales to the data-center market totaled $501 million for the fiscal third quarter, more than doubling in size year over year.
That beat Wall Street's forecast for the quarter. Nvidia's market value has doubled this year, after having tripled last year, as the company has masterfully positioned itself at the prime slot in the market for artificial intelligence chips. It has also made Nvidia one of the most expensive stocks on the chip market at more than 50 times forward earnings, which sets sky-high expectations the company can't always meet.
Such was the case three months ago, when the company's data-center sales missed some projections and sent the stock down initially. But the data-center gain was stronger this time, and its main business of selling graphics chips used in gaming PCs looks good as well.
Nvidia is now the third most valuable chip maker on the market, lagging behind only Intel and Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing. That sets a rather high bar, but the company has shown great proficiency thus far in meeting and beating it.
