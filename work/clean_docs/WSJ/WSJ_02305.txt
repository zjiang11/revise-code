Hillary Clinton and U.S. election officials said they are concerned disinformation generated and spread by artificial intelligence could threaten the 2024 presidential election.
Clinton, a former secretary of state and 2016 presidential candidate, said she thinks foreign actors like Russian President Vladimir Putin could use AI to interfere in elections in the U.S. and elsewhere.
"Anybody who's not worried is not paying attention," Clinton said Thursday at Columbia University, where election officials and tech executives discussed how AI could affect global elections.
Clinton said Putin tried to undermine her in the 2016 election by spreading disinformation on Facebook, Twitter and Snapchat. "What they did to me was primitive, and what we're talking about now is the leap in technology," she said.
The 2024 election cycle is the first where officials are preparing to respond on a broad scale to disinformation created and spread by AI. People could use AI to send out images, audio and videos that misinform voters about their polling place or voting day.
At Columbia, election officials said they were worried about deepfake videos -- deceptive videos designed to look real -- that could lead people to believe false information.
"Now we're facing an election cycle where those lies will be turbocharged through AI," said Jocelyn Benson, Michigan's secretary of state.
Clinton, Benson and other leaders said Thursday they wanted some sort of federal regulation over AI and elections.
