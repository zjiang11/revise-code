[Financial Analysis and Commentary]
Among the drawbacks to having a super-popular CEO: Even the good ones never last forever.
Frank Slootman fit that category. Prior to taking Snowflake public in 2020, he did the same for ServiceNow in 2012 and Data Domain in 2007. Both did well by investors. ServiceNow's public market value surged nearly fivefold during his tenure. And Data Domain was sold to EMC two years later for more than double its IPO price.
Hence, Slootman's departure was never going to be taken well. The 65-year-old announced his retirement during Snowflake's fiscal fourth-quarter report late Wednesday -- eight months after an analyst meeting where he denied reports that he was planning such a move. The news came with a disappointing product-revenue forecast for the new fiscal year. Snowflake's shares fell 18% on Thursday.
Slootman framed his decision as an effort to hand the reins over to someone with technical chops to lead it into the age of artificial intelligence. The new boss is Sridhar Ramaswamy, a former Google executive who joined Snowflake last year through its acquisition of his Neeva startup. "Snowflake needs hard-driving technologists to navigate the challenges the new world represents," Slootman said.
Wall Street sees Snowflake's forecast as part of an effort to give the new CEO an easier starting point. "We think Snowflake cleared the decks for Mr. Ramaswamy," wrote Macquarie analyst Fred Havemeyer.
Snowflake was running a bit hot, with its stock up more than 30% since its latest earnings report. That put it at a sharp premium to even pricey cloud-software companies, with a multiple of more than 20 times forward sales that was nearly four times the average of the BVP Nasdaq Emerging Cloud Index. "We continue to view Snowflake as a great company, just not as great as that implied in its stock price," wrote Guggenheim's John DiFucci, who maintains a sell rating on it. Snowflake's new boss has some warming up to do.
