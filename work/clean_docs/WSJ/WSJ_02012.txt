Synopsys is in talks to acquire Ansys in a move that would create a new design-software giant and mark one of the first big merger deals of the new year.
An acquisition of Ansys, which has a market value of nearly $30 billion, could be struck early in 2024, according to people familiar with the matter. The talks could still fall apart, and another suitor could emerge.
Canonsburg, Penn.-based Ansys makes software that helps predict how products will work in the real world, according to its website. It is used in industries ranging from aerospace to healthcare and automotive. The company booked revenue of roughly $2.1 billion in 2022.
Sunnyvale, Calif.-based Synopsys, which has a value of roughly $85 billion, makes software that engineers use to design and test silicon chips used in everything from smartphones to self-driving cars and other forms of artificial intelligence.
Its shares have run up more than 70% this year as investors pile into companies that could benefit from a boom in the use of artificial intelligence. Synopsys' customers include Nvidia, Advanced Micro Devices and Intel.
Shares of Ansys are up nearly 48% so far this year. The stock surged 18% Friday, after Bloomberg reported late Thursday that the company is weighing options, including a possible sale, after receiving takeover interest. It didn't say from whom.
The companies have joined forces in the past. In 2017, they announced a partnership to integrate Ansys' technologies with some of Synopsys'. It was meant to help overlapping customers of the two companies use their products more efficiently.
A deal would rank among the largest deals struck in recent months -- particularly in the technology sector, in which activity has been slow amid high interest rates and a crackdown by antitrust enforcers that has chilled big transactions.
