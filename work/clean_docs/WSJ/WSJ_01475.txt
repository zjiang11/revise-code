[Financial Analysis and Commentary]
In the universe that Microsoft envisions, there is a bot for that.
That is a deviation from the app-centered world of smartphones, where one of the world's most valuable companies is just a bit player. Less than 2% of the smartphones sold last year carried the Windows Phone operating system, according to IDC. That may drop below 1% in the next four years.
Microsoft can't afford to ignore the mobile world. It has taken some significant steps, like making its applications available on iPhone and Android devices and pouring resources into its cloud business. But it still needs to be a relevant platform for developers and it can't do that while relying on apps made for an operating system with a tiny share.
This is why Microsoft is trying to find new ways to keep developers engaged. The HoloLens augmented-reality headset is one. It is now shipping to developers. Microsoft also announced its latest effort on Wednesday dubbed "conversations as a platform," which harnesses artificial intelligence for developers to make "bots" that can perform many of the tasks of mobile apps, often through voice control.
Both are compelling but will likely take a long time to come to fruition. So Microsoft can't turn its back on Windows. Wall Street expects the company's personal-computing segment to drive more than 40% of annual revenue in coming years. The latest Windows 10 release is promising with the installed base up 35% in the last three months. For developers, that type of reception still computes.
