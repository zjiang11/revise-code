[Financial Analysis and Commentary]
Coming up with headlines that will draw readers in isn't just a preoccupation of journalists and editors, but in recent years of Wall Street analysts.
The migration of research to email and online platforms, and the sheer volume of it that investors receive, make it hard to stand out.
So while there is still a place for headlines like "Quarterly Update" and "Price Pressures Rising," to make a splash analysts will try for something with a little more pizazz, or clickbait.
That is where the problems start, as some of these highlights of analyst reports from the second quarter show.
"Charts of Darkness for Apocalypse Dow," from Bank of America Merrill Lynch. Yes, Joseph Conrad's 1899 novella "Heart of Darkness" was the inspiration for Francis Ford Coppola's "Apocalypse Now," that "chart" rhymes with "heart" and that "Dow" rhymes with "now." We get it.
"Keep Your Eyes on the Prize," from Evercore ISI. An innocuous title for a note on Blackstone Group, but also the title of song that rose to prominence during the civil-rights movement.
Blackstone chief executive Stephen Schwarzman took home at least $800 million in 2017.
"Getting high on the Fed's supply of reserves," from Citigroup. To be honest, if you are writing about the short-end notes market you probably need all the help you can get.
"Facebook 1Q18 Earnings: No Nipple Slips Here," from MoffettNathanson.
That title was inspired by a comment from Chief Executive Mark Zuckerberg about the ability of artificial intelligence to detect nudity better than it now spots hate speech.
