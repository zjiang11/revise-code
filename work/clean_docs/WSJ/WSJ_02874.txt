REDWOOD CITY, Calif. -- Oracle Corp. said it plans to launch in June an "Internet Weather Map" aimed at helping businesses better anticipate latency issues and outages caused by storms, earthquakes, state intervention or other network disruptions.
Beyond targeted cyberattacks, one of the biggest challenges that chief information officers face is the volatility of the internet, said Kyle York, general manager and vice president of business and product strategy for the Oracle Dyn Global Business Unit. This is becoming increasingly important as more workloads, tools and services shift to third-party web-enabled systems, he said.
Mr. York's team tracks ups and downs in internet performance globally, such as the surge in online traffic last year across Puerto Rico in the days before Hurricane Maria wreaked havoc.
Oracle's internet performance map involves gathering countless micro-data points on paths and routing on the internet world-wide, and feeding it into artificial-intelligence tools and machine learning
Mr. York served as chief strategy officer at web-service provider Dyn, which was acquired by Oracle last year as part of an effort to rapidly build out its cloud-computing business. Launched in 2001, Dyn was among a dozen or so companies operating domain-name systems, which help direct traffic across the internet.
