Web-search powerhouse Google took a break from its normal course of business on Saturday to disclose its ambitious effort to develop technology that lets cars drive themselves.
The company said on its blog that it believed the use of such technology could reduce traffic accident deaths world-wide by half, saving about 600,000 lives per year. Google hopes to reduce carbon emissions, too.
Sebastian Thrun, a Google engineer and a professor who leads a robotics and artificial intelligence research lab at Stanford University, said the company's automated cars already have driven a collective 224,000 kilometers, from the Golden Gate Bridge to Los Angeles to Lake Tahoe. "We think this is a first in robotics research," Mr. Thrun said.
The cars, which were manned by trained operators, used "video cameras, radar sensors and a laser range finder to 'see' other traffic, as well as detailed maps . . . to navigate the road ahead," he said.
The data collected by the cars is processed by the company's data centers. Local police departments were briefed on the project, the company said.
The company didn't say what it would do next or whether it would seek to eventually make money from the venture. But with $30 billion in cash reserves, Google can afford to push tech limits without always worrying about such things.
