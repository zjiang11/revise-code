A little-known game developer called Machine Zone Inc. is on the verge of raising a round of financing that would catapult it into the ranks of the most valuable start-ups in the world.
The developer of "Game of War: Fire Age," a popular mobile game with a built-in language translator, is in advanced talks with J.P. Morgan Chase & Co. about raising funding at a valuation of more than $3 billion, according to people familiar with the negotiations.
It got its start in 2008 as a developer of a dating app, but co-founders, led by Chief Executive Gabriel Leydon, shifted their focus to making strategy games for smartphones and changed the company's name to Machine Zone in 2012.
One of the unique attributes of "Game of War" is its built-in language translator that lets gamers whether in Brazil, Singapore or New Zealand share in the same quests and battles.
"This is what my goal is, to network the entire world together into one real-time environment," Mr. Leydon said in an interview last August. He declined to comment Wednesday.
The game relies on translation services powered by Google Inc. and Microsoft Corp., and uses a technology called machine learning to build its own dictionary of terms that are more specific to videogames.
The company predicts revenue this year of around $600 million, according to a person familiar with its business. Apple ranks the game at No. 3 on its list of "top grossing" game apps.
The Palo Alto, Calif., company previously raised only about $13 million from investors including Menlo Ventures, Anthos Capital and Baseline Ventures, according to Dow Jones VentureSource. The company has more than 150 employees, including alumni of top game makers like Electronic Arts Inc. and Activision Blizzard.
