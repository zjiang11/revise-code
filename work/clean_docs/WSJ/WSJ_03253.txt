Nielsen Holdings PLC appointed David Kenny as chief executive as the ratings and data giant continues to review its strategic options amid activist pressure to either sell or spin off parts of its assets.
Mr. Kenny comes from International Business Machines Corp., where he served as senior vice president of cognitive solutions.
The 57-year-old will join Nielsen on Dec. 3, succeeding veteran Mitch Barns, who has been at the helm since 2014 and said in July that he would retire at the end of the year.
A longtime ad and technology executive, Mr. Kenny joins Nielsen at a critical juncture. The company began a full strategic review of its business in September, amid pressure to sell itself from activist investor Elliott Management Corp., which took an 8.4% stake in the company earlier this year.
The New York-based research company, which has a market capitalization of $9.1 billion, has come under pressure over the past few years because of changes in consumers' media habits, which include the rise of video consumption on mobile devices.
Nielsen's business includes media and television ratings operations, which is dubbed its "watch" segment, and its "buy" division, which tracks purchases of groceries and other consumer products.
The buy segment has been a weak spot, hampered by the changing retail environment and challenges that consumer-product companies face from new consumer tastes and shopping habits. Nielsen's stock has declined about 30% over the past year.
"There has never been a more important time for measurement and analytics around media and to have a trusted intermediary," Mr. Kenny said in an interview.
Nielsen Executive Chairman James Attwood said Mr. Kenny was chosen for his understanding of advertisers and his experience in artificial intelligence, big data and cloud technologies.
