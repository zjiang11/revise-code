[Financial Analysis and Commentary]
Artificial intelligence has given Samsung Electronics a boost, but the South Korean semiconductor company still has a lot of catching up to do.
Samsung's stock rose roughly 3% Friday after preliminary results for the June quarter came in ahead of analysts' forecasts. Samsung expects operating profit of around 10.4 trillion won, the equivalent of $7.5 billion -- a huge jump from just 670 billion won a year earlier. The company will announce its full results later this month.
AI has helped buoy memory-chip prices, which were in a deep slump last year. Prices of the NAND type used for data storage were 15% to 20% higher in the second quarter than in the previous three months, while those of DRAM, used for short-term computer memory, increased 13% to 18%, according to TrendForce. Both are expected to rise a further 5% to 10% this quarter, the industry tracker says.
Samsung's shares have gained 58% since the end of 2022 -- impressive until you look at those of local peer SK Hynix, which have more than tripled.
The latter company has taken an early lead in an advanced technology that provides high-bandwidth memory or HBM, as it is virtually the only supplier to AI leader Nvidia.
Samsung, the world's largest memory-chip maker, is in the unusual position of trying to catch up. Nvidia is testing Samsung's next-generation HBM chip, called HBM3e, but SK Hynix started mass production of its rival product in March.
The AI frenzy will likely keep supply in the overall memory chip market tight. That is good news for Samsung, but it still needs to work harder to protect its leadership position.
