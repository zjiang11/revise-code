[Financial Analysis and Commentary]
Prosus is a growth investor whose best investment is its own discounted stock -- not a great setup for incoming Chief Executive Fabricio Bloisi.
The Amsterdam-listed company owns a roughly $114 billion stake in Chinese tech conglomerate Tencent, as well as a wide range of e-commerce companies, the most valuable of which is Brazilian food-delivery company iFood.
Annual results published Monday showed progress in improving the operational performance of the e-commerce portfolio, which delivered its first-ever annual profit. But valuations were less flattering, with an internal rate of return -- a key measure of performance in the venture-capital business -- of just 4%. The company called the result "unsatisfactory."
Prosus has missed out on the latest craze, generative artificial intelligence. It is using the technology to improve its operations, but it doesn't have its own GenAI business.
Bloisi becomes CEO of Prosus next month after running iFood, a company he acquired in 2013 for his Brazilian technology firm Movile. The hope is that he can spread some of what has made iFood successful to the rest of the Prosus portfolio.
Prosus's market value is roughly $95 billion, well below even the value of its Tencent shares. The valuation discount has forced Prosus to reorganize its portfolio in recent years, including a program to sell Tencent stock to buy back its own. So far, Prosus has repurchased more than one-fifth of its own shares, more than any other technology firm globally, it says. That has boosted its book value per share by 8%.
Yet its discount to estimated book value remains stubbornly high, at 38%. Until Prosus can boost its internal rates of return, it will have little reason to invest in much beyond its own stock.
Investors are bound to wonder what asset the company could sell next.
