It's as uncomplicated a case as "Kramer vs. Kramer" -- or maybe "Kramer vs. Cramer."
Over-the-top streaming service Hulu, owned by media giants Comcast and Disney, sent a cease-and-desist letter to a startup named Humu about a year and a half ago alleging the likelihood of confusion between the brands.
But you can't watch "The Handmaid's Tale," or any other show for that matter, on Humu. It is an artificial-intelligence firm out to make employees more productive.
Things seemed to be cooling down once Hulu learned what sort of company had the similar-sounding trademark. Now, though, Humu has issued a complaint for declaratory judgment barring Hulu from ever suing it, even if the company one day moves into consumer-oriented video.
Hulu claims its name derives from Mandarin and means gourd or "holder of precious things." Humu is a shortened version of "humuhumunukunukuapua'a," the name for a Hawaiian triggerfish.
While reverting to that mouthful clearly isn't an option, perhaps a judge can settle the dispute by deciding in favor of which company's lawyer can say it 10 times quickly.
