Merck KGaA reached a deal to buy Versum Materials Inc. for $53 a share, scuttling a merger with Entegris Inc.
Merck valued its purchase of Versum at 5.8 billion euros ($6.56 billion), and the German company said the deal strengthens its position in the semiconductor industry, which is expected to see a boost from growth in the use of artificial intelligence, big data and internet-of-things applications.
Merck had earlier proposed buying the Tempe, Ariz., semiconductor-materials company for $48 a share.
The German company initiated a hostile takeover after Versum initially said it wasn't interested in a deal. Shares in Versum have consistently traded above $48, signaling that investors were expecting a higher price.
Versum said last week it had accepted Merck's increased proposal of $53 a share, and Entegris didn't submit a counterproposal. Versum's board unanimously approved the transaction, Merck said.
"Apparently $53 is the magic number," Evercore ISI analysts wrote in a recent research note, adding that the deal would diversify the German company from the liquid-crystals business, which has long been under pressure.
Versum and Entegris had reached a deal in January to combine. Entegris, based in Billerica, Mass., said it would receive a $140 million deal-termination fee from Versum.
Versum has about 2,300 employees across sites in North America and Asia. Darmstadt, Germany-based Merck has more than 51,000 employees.
The deal is expected to close in the second half.
