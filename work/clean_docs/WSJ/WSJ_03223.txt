Shares of Tupperware, Rite Aid and trucking giant Yellow have been on a wild ride.
The eye-popping swings in recent days are reminiscent of the meme-stock craze during the pandemic that turned GameStop into an unlikely stock-market star, as traders rush into shares and options of distressed companies and stoke huge jumps with bets that they will defy the odds and cash in big.
The bets are often bold. Some of the most widely traded options tied to Tupperware this week were for the shares jumping to $6 or $7.50.
Tupperware shares, which had recently traded for less than $1, jumped above $5 this week before closing at $3.52 Thursday.
Shares of Yellow, which shut down on Sunday and said it intends to file for bankruptcy, also saw huge jumps this week before spiraling down.
Shares of another struggling company, Rite Aid, skyrocketed more than 30% on Wednesday before popping another 15% on Thursday.
The big swings show how enthusiasm is sweeping far corners of the stock market this year (though it diminished a bit after the U.S. downgrade), propelling shares of speculative artificial-intelligence companies and cryptocurrencies. Everyday traders also are jumping back into markets, particularly for some of the risky options trades that boomed during the pandemic.
Meme stocks also experienced a revival last summer, driving dizzying swings in stocks including Bed Bath & Beyond. Some traders had been hanging onto bets since 2021, waiting to pounce and cash in when volatility strikes.
The wild ride for meme stocks has been dotted with painful losses. Just recently, individual investors learned that they would be wiped out during Bed Bath & Beyond's restructuring.
Tupperware shares tumbled around 4.1% Thursday. Yellow shares jumped 10.1%.
