Stocks and bond yields rose ahead of a busy week of earnings, with some of the biggest U.S. corporations due to report.
After a jolt of volatility in the first quarter, April has brought a calm across a range of markets -- including stocks, bonds and currencies.
Stocks hugged the flatline for much of the trading session and turned higher in the final hour of the trading day, with gains accelerating into the closing bell. The S&P 500 rose 13.68 points, or 0.3%, to 4151.32. The Dow Jones Industrial Average added 100.71 points, or 0.3%, to 33987.18. The Nasdaq Composite added 34.26 points, or 0.3%, to 12157.72.
Swings for individual shares have been larger, particularly for some of the companies reporting results. While big banks last week kicked off earnings on a strong note, analysts expect overall S&P 500 earnings to decline for a second straight quarter and log the steepest quarterly decline since 2020. Tesla, Johnson & Johnson and Netflix are among the companies reporting results this week.
Results from regional banks will also be in the spotlight, with investors on the lookout for what executives say about last month's banking crisis and their lending standards moving forward.
Monday's stock-market moves highlighted how investors are closely parsing earnings reports for financial companies and quickly picking the winners and losers. Shares of M&T Bank rose $9.07, or 7.8%, to $125.66 after its earnings, making it the top gainer in the S&P 500 for the day.
Charles Schwab and State Street reported declines in customer deposits, a sign that interest rates are weighing on banks' balance sheets. Schwab still reported a higher quarterly profit and shares of the brokerage rose $2, or 3.9%, to $52.77.
Shares in State Street fell $7.35, or 9.2%, to $72.68, after it reported lower-than-expected earnings, making it the worst performer in the S&P 500.
"We have a pretty conservative view of earnings over the next 12 to 18 months," said Chris Marangi, co-chief investment officer of value for Gabelli Funds.
Investors are also fixated on the path of interest-rate increases, with many now anticipating another increase at the Federal Reserve's May meeting -- a turnaround from a month ago when stress in the banking industry fueled bets the Fed would have to cut its rate-raising cycle short. Fed comments last week put rate increases back on the table.
The 10-year Treasury yield rose for the third consecutive session, to 3.590% from 3.521% Friday. The 10-year yield has risen for seven of the past eight trading days.
"I think the market has come to terms with the fact that the Fed will be hiking again in May," said Jane Foley, head of foreign-exchange strategy at Rabobank.
In corporate news, Alphabet shares slid 2.7% after the New York Times reported that Google was devising radical search changes to beat back artificial-intelligence rivals.
Brent crude, the international oil benchmark, shed 1.8% to $84.76 a barrel.
The tepid stock moves extended to Europe. The Stoxx Europe 600 lost less than 0.1%. Early Tuesday, Japan's Nikkei 225 was up 0.6%, but Hong Kong's Hang Seng Index was down 0.7% and the Shanghai Composite was down 0.1% after China reported that its economy grew by 4.5% in the first three months of the year. S&P 500 futures were flat.
