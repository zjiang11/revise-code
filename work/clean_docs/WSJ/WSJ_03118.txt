Intel has added Microsoft as a customer for a contract chip-making arm it started three years ago as part of chief executive Pat Gelsinger's bid to revitalize the company.
Microsoft is designing chips to be made in one of Intel's most advanced factories, CEO Satya Nadella said at an Intel event. Nadella didn't specify which chip Intel would make, but Microsoft has sought to bolster its chip-design capabilities in recent months, including a new chip for artificial-intelligence calculations it unveiled last year.
Intel also gave an update on its chip-making technology, announcing a new advancement in chip manufacturing it expects to come online in 2027.
The company has been pushing to make five chip advancements in four years following Gelsinger's appointment in 2021 and on Wednesday said it remains on track to meet that objective, which includes making the world's most cutting-edge chips by next year.
Intel's contract chip-making business has a long way to go before catching up to the two largest players in the industry, Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing and Samsung Electronics. Intel has set an internal goal of being the second-biggest contract chip maker by 2030.
The unit, called Intel Foundry, reported $291 million of revenue in its latest quarter. The company said there was much potential, though, touting $15 billion of total "lifetime deal value" for the business.
