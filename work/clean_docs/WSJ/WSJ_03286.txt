Cognizant Technology Solutions Corp. was founded in 1994 as the India-based technology unit of Dun & Bradstreet and spun off in a public offering in 1998. Since then, it has grown into a Fortune 500 company that provides information-technology services from labor outsourcing and systems integration to application development and consulting.
The company, with a market cap of $34.6 billion, is based in Teaneck, N.J., but most of its employees are in India.
Activist investor Elliott Management Corp. said Monday it held a 4% stake in Cognizant worth $1.4 billion and called for the company to increase profitability rather than focusing on revenue growth.
IT-services firms like Cognizant have struggled with a broader industry trend of companies looking beyond cheap labor and instead prioritizing technology-driven business models that can generate new revenue.
The shift has prompted outsourcing firms to focus less on traditional IT services and create new digital offerings that flow from cloud computing, data analytics and automation.
Cognizant has emphasized its own shift to digital services in recent years, and has invested in artificial intelligence technologies that help automate business processes. The firm has made astring of acquisitions in recent months aimed at boosting its digital services portfolio, such as Norwegian IT services firm Frontica Business Solutions. Cognizant also has worked on several projects focused on helping companies generate revenue. It said in October it would help Key Corp. subsidiary KeyBank deploy a digital banking platform.
Traditional lines of business grew by less than 1% over the past 12 months at the 20 largest publicly traded IT services providers, including Cognizant, according to research from Everest Group and DeepDive Equity Research. Cloud and digital-services revenue at those companies grew 22% during the same period.
