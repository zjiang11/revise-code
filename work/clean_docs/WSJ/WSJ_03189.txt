The Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped Wednesday, weighed down by a slide in the shares of International Business Machines after the tech giant posted its 20th consecutive quarter in which revenue fell from a year earlier.
The Dow industrials were off 54 points, or 0.3%, at 20469 in midday trading. The S&P 500 was up 0.2% and the Nasdaq Composite gained 0.6%, as investors digested more earnings reports.
Shares of IBM lost 5% after the company late Tuesday reported a 13% drop in quarterly profit and disappointing revenue. It posted narrower profit margins across its business units, including divisions that manage the cloud-computing and Watson artificial intelligence operations that IBM is banking on to transform its business.
The financial sector in the S&P 500 index rose 0.2% after Morgan Stanley said its quarterly earnings climbed 70%. The New York-based firm was up 2.6%.
Investors' allocation to U.S. stocks fell to its lowest levels since January 2008 in a survey of global fund managers by Bank of America Merrill Lynch, with 83% saying U.S. stocks are overvalued -- the highest on record.
In Asia, Japan's Nikkei Stock Average edged up 0.1%. China's Shanghai Composite Index fell 0.8% to its lowest level since February, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng Index shed 0.4%.
Australia's S&P/ASX 200 fell 0.6%.
