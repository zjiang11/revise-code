Private-equity firms that previously backed supply-chain management software company o9 Solutions chipped in more capital to help the business expand, raising its valuation by more than a third to $3.7 billion as demand surges for business applications based on artificial-intelligence technology.
Firms including General Atlantic, Generation Investment Management and KKR & Co. added $116 million to their growth commitments to Dallas-based o9, whose clients such as Walmart and Budweiser brewer Anheuser-Busch InBev use its AI programs to forecast demand, manage procurement and avoid supply-chain glitches.
While the firms increased their investments in o9, only General Atlantic raised its ownership stake, according to a company spokeswoman. The new deal relies on a 37% higher valuation, or $1 billion more than when General Atlantic and Generation Investment first backed the company early last year. General Atlantic invested in o9 from its sustainability-focused BeyondNetZero fund.
Rapid advances in AI technology -- particularly generative AI, which creates new content -- have excited investors. Capital commitments to startups focused on developing applications using AI have risen around 38% to more than $4 billion this year, excluding Microsoft's $10 billion deal to back ChatGPT developer OpenAI, according to research provider CB Insights.
Researchers have cited a rapidly expanding market for AI tools applied to supply chains.
