Schneider Electric removed Peter Herweck from his role as chief executive after just 18 months on the job, citing differences on the strategic road map, and replaced him with company veteran Olivier Blum.
The French energy-management and automation group said the appointment is effective immediately.
On a call with analysts after the appointment was announced on Monday, Schneider Electric Chief Financial Officer Hilary Maxson said the board felt the strategic road map wasn't being executed quickly enough or in a decisive and collaborative manner, Stifel analyst Alexander Wahl said in a note.
Herweck couldn't be reached for a comment.
Analysts said the news was unexpected but unlikely to be a drag in the long term.
"Today's announcement is a surprise, not least given the brevity of Herweck's tenure," RBC Europe analyst Mark Fielding said. He said that while the move didn't inspire confidence, Blum was a logical choice to succeed Herweck, and that it was surprising Blum wasn't named CEO 18 months ago.
Blum has been on the company's executive committee since 2014 and most recently oversaw Schneider Electric's energy management business, the company said. He joined the company in 1993.
"While the company stresses no near-term operational issue, the decision will likely be seen as a clear surprise and could weigh on the shares as the market digests the news, although the long-term impact may ultimately not be negative," Citi analysts said in a note.
Schneider Electric shares fell 2.3% in Paris Monday.
Last year, Schneider Electric said it was focusing on trends such as artificial intelligence and climate change to drive growth.
Between 2024 and 2027, it is targeting organic revenue growth of between 7% to 10% and organic growth of adjusted earnings before interest, taxes and amortization margin of around half a percentage point, it has said.
The company reported record third-quarter revenue at the end of October.
