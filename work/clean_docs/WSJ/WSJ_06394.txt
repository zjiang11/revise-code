[Financial Analysis and Commentary]
SoftBank's fortunes increasingly depend on British chip designer Arm.
Nearly half of the Japanese technology investor's net asset value now comes from Arm, SoftBank's calculations showed Monday. The market's view is even more extreme, valuing SoftBank's 90% stake at $100 billion, higher than SoftBank's market value of $75 billion.
Arm's shares have more than doubled since its Nasdaq initial public offering in September. That helped push SoftBank's estimated net asset value up 45% last quarter. The weak yen also gave a lift as most of SoftBank's investments are outside of Japan.
SoftBank's other investments didn't do as well. Its Vision Fund unit reported an investment loss of around 115 billion yen, equivalent to $740 million, for the quarter.
SoftBank now sees Arm at the core of its artificial intelligence strategy. This makes sense: As data centers use more power, they will likely increasingly adopt Arm's energy-efficient design. Cloud giants Amazon.com and Microsoft have both launched server chips based on Arm's architecture.
But Arm's growth prospects are well understood: Its shares trade at 70 times expected earnings, according to FactSet.
SoftBank's stock offers a cheap way in, but then investors need to worry about the Japanese company's patchy record with capital allocation.
