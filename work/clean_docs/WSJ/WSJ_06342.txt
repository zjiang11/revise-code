[Financial Analysis and Commentary]
Investors have hit the reset button on chip stocks a few times this year. But the group is still running a bit hot -- and not only because of artificial-intelligence hype.
This week has proven just the latest example. Tuesday's market selloff hit the chip sector hard, sending the PHLX Semiconductor Index down 7.8% for the day and taking AI titan Nvidia down 9.5%. That turned out to be the worst session in more than four years for the so-called SOX index, though it was the fifth time the group shed more than 5% in a single session since mid-July, according to FactSet data.
That isn't a coincidence. The SOX index hit an all-time closing high on July 10 that represented a 42% year-to-date gain, which followed a 65% jump in 2023.
The run-up during the first half of the year put the index's average multiple to around 36 times forward earnings, which is the richest multiple the group has carried since 2009, when it was emerging from the financial crisis. At that level, chip stocks on the index were averaging a 67% premium to the S&P 500, according to FactSet data.
That premium has shrunk to about 30% now. Tuesday's selloff brings the SOX index's year-to-date performance roughly in line with the S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite, which may have investors thinking the damage is done.
The group regained some ground Wednesday morning, led by Advanced Micro Devices. The chip maker better known as AMD saw its stock jump 2.9% on Wednesday, likely helped by a report from Bloomberg that the Justice Department has subpoenaed rival Nvidia as part of a possible antitrust investigation into its dominant AI business.
