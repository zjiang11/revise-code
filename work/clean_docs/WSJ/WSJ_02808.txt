Shares of SenseTime hit a record low Monday after the Chinese artificial-intelligence specialist said its controlling shareholder, billionaire academic Tang Xiao'ou, had died.
Tang passed away on Dec. 15 due to health issues, the company said in an exchange filing on Sunday. The obituary posted on the company website didn't specify his age or the type of illness.
Widely seen as a pioneer in AI, Tang earned his doctorate from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology and taught at a Hong Kong university.
"The passing of Prof. Tang is not expected to have a material adverse impact on the daily management and the ordinary business activities of the Company," SenseTime said in another exchange filing on Monday, before the market opened.
The company is losing money. It reported a 1.3% rise in revenue from a year ago to $201.4 million for the first half of this financial year.
The news of Tang's death came weeks after U.S. short seller Grizzly Research accused the Hong Kong-based company of inflating its revenue. In response to the allegation, SenseTime said the report "contains unfounded allegations, misleading conclusions and interpretations," according to an exchange filing.
The U.S. government has sanctioned SenseTime repeatedly since 2019 for allegedly violating human rights in Xinjiang. The company denied the accusations and listed in Hong Kong in late 2021.
SenseTime's investors include China's Mixed-Ownership Reform Fund. SenseTime develops technologies, including facial recognition, image recognition, autonomous driving, with the use of artificial intelligence.
At the time of his death, Tang had a net worth of $1.1 billion, according to Forbes.
