TOKYO -- The co-founder of SenseTimeGroup Ltd., a Chinese artificial-intelligence startup under fire for the use of its facial-recognition technology in mass-surveillance systems, said its technology should be regulated rather than banned.
SenseTime, which has received funding from the Saudi-backed SoftBank Vision Fund, is navigating growing privacy concerns as government agencies and companies around the world look to artificial intelligence to store and analyze data of people's faces.
Last week, San Francisco became the first U.S. city to ban the use offacial recognition by local agencies, in response to criticism that the technology gives authorities excessive means to spy on the public and erode civil liberties.
"This is useful, meaningful debate on what policies and regulations should be in place so that these new pieces of technology wouldn't bring damage," Bing Xu, co-founder of SenseTime, said at The Wall Street Journal's CEO Council conference in Tokyo on Tuesday.
The company said it recently exited a police technology venture in Xinjiang, where its technology is used in mass surveillance of ethnic Muslims. The pullout is temporary while the company ascertains the situation in Xinjiang, Mr. Xu said.
He defended the use of facial recognition as a means to make large cities safer, cleaner, and more efficient. "We are thinking about the public good as well as the individual," Mr. Xu said.
United Nations officials say China's Communist Party has detained as many as one million Turkic Muslims, most of them Uighurs, in internment camps in Xinjiang. China's leaders dispute the one-million figure and call the camps vocational training centers, saying it is a means to combat violent extremism in the region.
The use of facial recognition software to authenticate transactions will be everywhere, Mr. Xu said, likening the technology to a utility like electricity.
"Everything has two sides," he said.
