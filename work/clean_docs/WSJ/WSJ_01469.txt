"That's why I'm not afraid of artificial intelligence -- people will always pay someone to get stuff wrong."
Thus quipped one of the eminent economists gathered in rural Maine last weekend for a fishing trip dubbed Camp Kotok but also known as the "shadow Fed."
A highlight of the annual event, which was started 25 years ago by David Kotok, chairman and chief investment officer at investment firm Cumberland Advisors, is a betting pool on an array of economic indicators.
Typically a lowlight of the weekend is the unveiling of the results of the previous year's betting pool. Among the notable misses was the group's average forecast for the 10-year Treasury note yield at the end of July: 2.3% compared with the actual 1.45%, and the predicted yen-dollar exchange rate of 128.73 versus 102.4.
Another prediction, the federal-funds rate at the end of 2016, isn't looking too likely either: 0.92%. Most of the economists now view multiple rate rises in 2016 as the one that got away.
The group's expectation for the 10-year note a year from now is 1.61% and the yen 113. A plurality don't expect another Fed rate increase until at least 2017.
John Mousseau of Cumberland Advisors won last year's pool and has come in first or second about half the time in the past decade. His secret: Get a sense of how the group feels after a few days of conversation and fishing and go the other way, much as one would in a thoroughbred horse race.
"Except I'm betting against thoroughbred economists and money managers."
