Activist hedge-fund manager Elliott Investment Management is making a play at global tech investor SoftBank Group -- again.
Elliott, founded by billionaire Paul Singer, has amassed a roughly $2 billion stake in Masayoshi Son's SoftBank in a play to prod the company to spend its cash on a major stock buyback, according to a person familiar with the matter.
It's a familiar move. Four years ago, Elliott built a $2.5 billion stake in the Tokyo company, and urged Son to sell many of its tech holdings and undertake a buyback.
Within months, SoftBank launched a more than $20 billion share buyback and began selling more than $40 billion of its holdings in Alibaba and other companies. Buoyed by the pandemic tech boom, SoftBank's stock hit all-time highs in 2021, surpassing its dotcom-era peak for the first time.
Elliott now wants SoftBank to buy back another $15 billion of stock, the person said.
At issue: SoftBank is worth far more as a sum of its parts. By its own count, SoftBank's holdings -- most of which come from its roughly 90% stake in chip design company Arm -- were worth $178 billion as of March 31. That dwarfs SoftBank's market capitalization of $88 billion.
SoftBank shares jumped 4.6% after the Financial Times reported the stake.
While SoftBank has had a long series of misses in recent years with its Vision Fund startup investments and a big bet on WeWork, the stock market sun has again been shining on Son.
SoftBank shares are up 50% since the start of the year, within spitting distance of the 2021 highs. That is largely thanks to investor hunger for artificial intelligence-related stocks, which has helped push shares in Arm up 82% this year.
