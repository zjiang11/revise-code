[Financial Analysis and Commentary]
Big parties tend to run up big bills. For many chip stocks, that bill is coming due.
Semiconductors have been the best-performing tech sector this year by far. At its peak this past week, the PHLX Semiconductor Index was up more than 34% for the year to a 16-year high. The group even managed a strong postelection boost -- unlike the rest of the tech sector. By the close of November, the index was up 6% since the vote, outperforming even the Dow industrials.
But nothing lasts forever. A harsh selloff Thursday clipped much of the recent gains. Citigroup noted that investors are rotating out of chips and into financials and energy, the latter helped by the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries' production cut. And while the group was faring better Friday, investors should expect more volatility ahead, given the recent run-up and strong potential for a slowdown in mergers and acquisitions.
Still, before the next rush of sell calls, consider that the chip sector isn't exactly overvalued. Strong earnings expansion has kept valuation in check. The PHLX is averaging about 14.6 times forward earnings, which is up only 7% since the start of the year and 27% below the average of the Nasdaq Composite.
Nvidia, whose shares have more than doubled this year and now trades above 30 times forward earnings, seems an easy target to take some profits. But the company has taken up a strong position in automotive and deep learning -- two major growth segments for the years ahead.
That sort of promise should encourage investors to keep some chips in the game.
