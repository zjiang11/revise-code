[Financial Analysis and Commentary]
Nvidia is pushing to new heights of advanced computing. That should help investors acclimate to the thin air around the stock.
Known for graphics processors used in high-powered PCs, Nvidia is upping its game. At a users' conference last week, it shared how its chips are powering artificial-intelligence applications, as well as cloud-computing data centers. Cars also are a big focus, as assisted- and automated-driving features require the type of graphical processing power Nvidia specializes in.
Those efforts are bearing fruit. While the auto business is only about 6% of revenue, Nvidia said it grew 75% in the fiscal year ended Jan. 31. And the gaming business that produces most of Nvidia's current sales could get additional lift from virtual-reality headsets.
The catch: At around 25 times forward earnings, the stock is one of the most expensive among large-cap chip shares. And royalty payments from Intel of $264 million, or 43% of net income, in fiscal 2016 sunset next year. A new deal is possible, though Nvidia has told investors not to count on it.
The rich valuation and loss of a revenue stream could put the brakes on the stock, up 60% the past year. But investors shouldn't panic. Nvidia's multiple isn't so rich versus other tech plays exposed to hot markets. Mobileye, which makes chip products for automated driving, is at more than 55 times. Plus, adjusted for more than $3 billion in net cash, Nvidia's multiple drops to 20 times.
Nvidia is running hot, but it won't blow a gasket.
