A selloff in technology shares drove major stock indexes lower, while traders awaited quarterly results from Nvidia, the chip maker at the center of the market frenzy around artificial intelligence.
The Nasdaq Composite led the way down, dropping 1.1%. The S&P 500 shed 0.6%. The Dow Jones Industrial Average, which hit a record high on Tuesday, declined 0.4%, or 159 points.
Nvidia's profit and sales more than doubled in the second quarter from a year earlier, topping Wall Street forecasts. Its shares, which were up more than 150% for the year, declined in after-hours trading immediately following the release of its results, extending a 2.1% slide during the regular session.
Irene Tunkel, chief U.S. equities strategist at BCA Research, said Wednesday's trading suggested many nervous traders are eager to lock in gains on technology stocks such as Nvidia.
"The reason we have profit taking is lots of people made lots of money," she said. "How greedy do you have to be?"
Super Micro Computer, which makes specialized servers using Nvidia chips, gave investors other reasons to worry about a bubble in AI stocks deflating. Supermicro tumbled 19% Wednesday after it said it needed more time to file its annual financial statement. A day earlier, short-seller Hindenburg Research said it was betting against the company.
Retail and apparel companies reported a mixed bag of results. The good: Department store owner Nordstrom added 4.2% and pet-food delivery company Chewy jumped 11% after topping Wall Street's sales expectations.
Shopping mall staples Foot Locker and Abercrombie & Fitch tumbled 10% and 17%, respectively, after their results disappointed investors.
