Facebook Inc. on Wednesday reported an 11% increase in third-quarter profit, despite spending heavily on projects as varied as artificial intelligence, virtual reality and bringing Internet access to emerging markets.
Revenue rose 41% to $4.5 billion, from $3.2 billion in the same period a year earlier, as companies spent more to advertise across the social network. But total costs and expenses increased an even faster 68%.
The results reflect the progress Facebook has made in attracting ad dollars, particular for video ads.
Advertising-technology company SocialCode says its clients' spending on Facebook video ads tripled during the first three quarters of 2015 compared with the same period in 2014.
Shares of Facebook rose 2.2% to $106.20 in after-hours trading.
In the third quarter, Facebook made it easier for companies to buy ads on the Instagram photo-sharing app and reach its 400 million users.
Evercore ISI analyst Ken Sena estimated that by the fourth quarter, Instagram could contribute as much as $300 million, or 5% of Facebook's revenue.
For the third quarter, Facebook reported profit of $896 million, or 32 cents a share, up from $806 million, or 30 cents a share reported a year ago.
Analysts expected Facebook to report third-quarter profit of $776.5 million, or 27 cents a share, according to data compiled by S&P Capital IQ.
Excluding some expenses, Facebook reported earnings of 57 cents a share, above the 52 cents a share projected by Wall Street analysts.
Facebook's operating margin slipped to 54% from 57%.
