BEIJING--Alibaba Group Holding Ltd.'s cloud-computing unit is increasing its big data and artificial intelligence capabilities, trying to gain scale in a fast-growing market.
As part of that push, Alibaba Cloud, or AliCloud, has hired as its chief scientist Zhou Jingren, a Microsoft Corp. veteran who worked on the Bing search infrastructure team and big data unit. Mr. Zhou will lead AliCloud research teams in Hangzhou, Beijing and Seattle, developing platforms to help business customers analyze big data, the company said.
AliCloud, which currently has 500,000 paying customers for its storage, security, database management and other cloud-computing services, aims to expand its user base and offerings. The demand for cloud-based services is expanding rapidly in China and is projected to reach $20 billion by 2020, up from $1.5 billion in 2013, according to management consulting firm Bain & Co.
Globally, Alibaba is competing with bigger cloud-computing rivals Amazon.com Inc. and Microsoft. Morgan Stanley puts a valuation of $39 billion on AliCloud, compared with $91 billion for Amazon Web Services, but in a recent research note said AliCloud could become one of Alibaba's "most significant value drivers" by 2020. The bank increased its revenue projection for AliCloud by 24% for the current fiscal year ending March 2017.
One of the issues facing AliCloud is how secure its services are in a country where government operates with few checks and . Alibaba has said it doesn't share individual data, and that customers of AliCloud own their data outright, even when using the company's platform.
Mr. Zhou said in an interview that his department will focus on helping businesses leverage and analyze their big data, in part by providing a platform that can offer customized solutions. For example, a company's customer-service division could use AliCloud's tools to monitor recorded phone calls between consumers and customer-service representatives to detect problems and patterns, such as whether a representative raised his voice or if the consumer was dissatisfied with a solution.
