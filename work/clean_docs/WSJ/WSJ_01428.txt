[Financial Analysis and Commentary]
SoftBank Group has lain low during the past year as plunging technology stocks sank the company into two straight years of losses. The latest frenzy in artificial intelligence, however, has got its flamboyant chairman, Masayoshi Son, excited again.
The AI boom could bring real benefits to the company, but SoftBank needs to be careful not to dive headlong into another rash investment spree.
On Wednesday, Son said it would soon be time for SoftBank "to go on the counteroffensive." The larger-than-life boss of the Japanese tech-investment company has long talked about an AI revolution -- and he is clearly fired up by the success of ChatGPT.
SoftBank could indeed benefit from the surging use of AI. Its stock jumped 37% since Nvidia, another obvious beneficiary of the AI mania, last month forecast record profit due to strong demand for its cutting-edge chips. U.K. chip designer Arm, acquired by SoftBank in 2016 for $32 billion, will be a winner as companies invest more in computational firepower. SoftBank has a 75% direct stake in Arm, with the rest owned by its Vision Fund.
Nvidia, the undisputed leader in AI chips, tried to acquire Arm to create the "world's premier computing company for the age of AI" -- as the deal announcement put it -- in 2020. The transaction fell apart last year as it failed to gain regulators' nod.
Still, the two companies' symbiotic relationship continues to be a notable feature of the chip world. Nvidia plans to start shipping its Grace server chips, based on Arm's architecture, this year in a bid to challenge Intel and Advanced Micro Devices in central processors for data centers.
The rally in Nvidia lifted other chip stocks: The PHLX Semiconductor Sector Index surged 44% this year. That bodes well for Arm's coming initial public offering. Jefferies -- which this month upgraded SoftBank to buy on its AI links -- raised its estimate of Arm's valuation to $60 billion from $40 billion. SoftBank's market value is around $72 billion. The tech rally should help SoftBank's Vision Fund, which has nursed losses for the past few quarters.
There is one big worry: Son's aggressive investment style could return in spades as optimism for AI reaches new heights. SoftBank threw tons of money into startups two years ago at high valuations during the pandemic, only to end up with hefty losses later.
SoftBank is a good bet to ride on the AI wave. But a more disciplined approach to its investment decisions -- even during periods of tech-sector optimism -- would be even better for shareholders over the long term.
