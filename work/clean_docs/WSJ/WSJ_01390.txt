Facebook Inc. Wednesday posted more than $1 billion in quarterly net income for the first time, reflecting the social network's ability to quickly capitalize on its popularity.
Fourth-quarter revenue rose 52% to $5.84 billion, up from $3.85 billion in the same period a year earlier, as the social network drew more interest from advertisers. Costs, meanwhile, rose 21%.
Its shares jumped 7.2% to $101.25 in after-hours trading.
Last year marked a big investment period for Facebook, which hired thousands and poured money into projects including its virtual-reality arm Oculus VR, artificial intelligence and extending Internet access to remote areas.
For the fourth quarter, Facebook reported net income of $1.56 billion, or 54 cents a share, up from $701 million, or 25 cents a share, a year ago.
Excluding some expenses, Facebook reported earnings of 79 cents a share, higher than the 68 cents a share projected by Wall Street analysts.
About 1.59 billion people accessed Facebook at least once a month in the fourth quarter.
