To the Editor:
In "The Cost of Keeping Children Poor" (Op-Ed, April 16), Mark R. Rank estimates that childhood poverty cost the country $1.03 trillion in 2015 and that the country could save at least $7 for every dollar invested in these children. His estimate is very close to that made by the Nobel laureate economist James Heckman that quality birth-to-5 early childhood programs deliver a 13 percent return on investment.
Careful estimates of the benefits of the Nurse Family Partnership in multiple sites provide further confirmation. N.F.P. brings nurses to poor first-time mothers from six months before birth until the child turns 2.
The payoff is only going to increase as artificial intelligence, robots and other technologies place further premiums on cognitive skills. Close to a million of the four million babies born every year in the United States today enter life in poverty. Without help, the chance that they will remain poor will increase during the 20 years before they begin to engage with the evolving economy.
Whether they will be net contributors to our common well-being depends on what we do now.
ARNOLD PACKER, SAN DIEGO
The writer was an assistant secretary of labor in the Carter administration and chief economist at the Senate Budget Committee, 1974-77.
