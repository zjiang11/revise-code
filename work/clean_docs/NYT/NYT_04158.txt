Stocks closed broadly lower on Wall Street Tuesday as tensions between the United States and China flared ahead of negotiations aimed at resolving the costly trade war between the world's two biggest economies.
The sell-off, which accelerated in the last hour of regular trading, knocked more than 300 points off the Dow Jones industrial average. Technology companies, banks and health care stocks bore the brunt of the selling, which stretched the market's losses further into a fourth week.
The market slide began after the United States blacklisted a group of Chinese companies, claiming that their technology was playing a role in the repression of China's Muslim minority groups. The State Department also imposed restrictions on visas for Chinese officials.
The moves cast more doubt on whether Washington and Beijing would find a resolution to their long-running and economically damaging trade conflict. Envoys from the United States and China are scheduled to meet in Washington on Thursday for another round of trade talks.
The S&P 500 index lost 45.73 points, or 1.6 percent, to 2,893.06. The Dow slid 313.98 points, or 1.2 percent, to 26,164.04. The Nasdaq, heavily weighted with technology companies, dropped 132.52 points, or 1.7 percent, to 7,823.78. Smaller company stocks were also big decliners, sending the Russell 2000 index down 25.19 points, or 1.7 percent, to 1,472.60.
The yield on the 10-year Treasury fell to 1.53 percent from 1.57 percent Monday, a signal that investors favored lower-risk investments amid the trade war turmoil. Utilities and real estate companies, both safe-play sectors, held up better than the rest of the market, though they also ended the day in the red.
Technology stocks were among the biggest losers as chip makers absorbed the impact of the latest United States restrictions on sales to Chinese tech companies that develop facial recognition and other artificial intelligence technology. Ambarella tumbled 9.5 percent, and Nvidia fell 3.9 percent. The sector has shouldered much of the volatility from swings in trade war sentiment because many of the companies face bigger risks to sales and supply chains.
Several medical device makers knocked down health care stocks. Thermo Fisher fell 6 percent, and Boston Scientific slid 6.1 percent. Banks dropped as bond yields fell. Bank of America lost 2.4 percent.
Benchmark crude oil fell 12 cents to settle at $ 52.63 a barrel. Brent crude oil, the international standard, slid 11 cents to close at $ 58.24 a barrel. While the price of U.S. crude is up just under 9 percent so far this year, it remains off by more than 27 percent from a year ago. That slide has weighed on energy stocks this year.
Energy is the biggest loser among the S&P 500's 11 sectors. It is down 2.8 percent for the year and 27.8 percent over the past 12 months. It's also the worst-performing sector so far this month.
The stocks could face more pressure when energy companies report their third-quarter results later this month. The sector is expected to report a nearly 10 percent drop in revenue for the third quarter due to a 19 percent decline in crude oil prices from a year ago, according to Credit Suisse analyst Jonathan Golub.
The analyst expects the sector to reduce earnings growth for the S&P 500 by 1.9 percent for the third quarter.
Wholesale gasoline rose a penny to $ 1.58 per gallon. Heating oil added 1 cent to $ 1.91 per gallon. Natural gas fell 2 cents to $ 2.29 per 1,000 cubic feet.
Gold slipped 50 cents to $ 1,497.20 per ounce.
The dollar fell to 107.08 Japanese yen from 107.24 yen on Monday. The euro weakened to 1.0955 from $ 1.0974.
Major stock indexes in Europe finished broadly lower after the British government warned that chances of a separation deal with the European Union were fading.
