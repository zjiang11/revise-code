Silicon Valley's Top 10 companies employed lower percentages of women, African-Americans and Hispanics in 2005 than in 1999, a recent analysis by The San Jose Mercury News showed.
Part of the issue may be the overall drop in students majoring in computer science nationwide. Rather than curse the empty seats in computer science classes, Mehran Sahami, a Stanford University professor and associate chairman for education in the computer science department, led an effort to make the subject more appealing.
The department consolidated the core required courses to 6, down from 15. It also let students specialize in related subfields like artificial intelligence and computer graphics and let classes in other areas like human-computer interactions and studio arts count toward their major requirements.
After the first year, the number of students choosing that major jumped 40 percent, to 123. That trend continues this year with another 20 percent increase, Mr. Sahami said. A higher percentage of women than before are choosing to major in computer science.
