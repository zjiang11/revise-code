As it aims to build driverless cars and a new generation of robots, Google has agreed to acquire the British artificial intelligence developer DeepMind.
A Google spokesman confirmed the deal on Monday but declined to discuss the price or to elaborate on how Google plans to use DeepMind's projects, which include applications for e-commerce and games.
DeepMind was founded by Demis Hassabis, a computer game designer, neuroscientist and former child chess prodigy, and his partners Shane Legg and Mustafa Suleyman.
DeepMind, based in London, describes itself as combining "the best techniques from machine learning and systems neuroscience to build powerful general-purpose learning algorithms."
ReCode, the digital news venture started by Walter S. Mossberg and Kara Swisher, the founders of All Things Digital, reported the deal on Sunday, saying Google had agreed to pay $400 million for the firm. Some British news reports on Monday cited somewhat higher figures, without identifying their sources.
The deal is the latest in a series of acquisitions for Google, which has evolved from an Internet search and email provider into a firm that also develops driverless cars and mobile phones.
Last year, it purchased seven companies related to the development of advanced robotics, including Boston Dynamics, and Makani Power, which makes airborne wind turbines. This month, Google announced that it would buy Nest Labs, which makes thermostats and smoke alarms that are connected to the Internet and learn a homeowner's preferences, for $3.2 billion.
