* Alvin Ailey American Dance Theater (through June 21) The company's spring season at Lincoln Center continues with the return of "Odetta," Matthew Rushing's tribute to the legendary folk singer Odetta Holmes that premiered in December (Friday and Sunday afternoon). Also joining the season is Ulysses Dove's crisp and lusty "Bad Blood" (Saturday evening and Sunday afternoon), Wayne McGregor's glassy "Chroma" (Tuesday and Thursday) and Hofesh Shechter's dark and stormy "Uprising" (Saturday evening). Additional company repertory rounds out the programs. Friday at 8 p.m., Saturday at 2 and 8 p.m., Sunday at 3 and 7:30 p.m., Tuesday through Thursday at 7:30 p.m., David H. Koch Theater, Lincoln Center, 212-496-0600, ailey.org. (Brian Schaefer)
* American Ballet Theater (through July 4) The handsomely vintage new production of "The Sleeping Beauty" by Alexei Ratmansky gets three more performances this season (Friday night and two on Saturday). Beginning Monday, Ballet Theater welcomes back one of its staples: Kenneth MacMillan's 1965 production of "Romeo and Juliet" (through June 20). It has all the wonderment, passion and visual elegance of Franco Zeffirelli's classic film (which it predates), with the added benefit of Prokofiev's arresting score. Mondays through Fridays at 7:30 p.m., Saturdays at 8 p.m., matinees on Wednesdays and Saturdays at 2 p.m., Metropolitan Opera House, Lincoln Center, 212-362-6000, abt.org. (Schaefer)
Ballet Tech Kids Dance (through Sunday) For almost 20 years, Eliot Feld's Ballet Tech Foundation has partnered with the New York City Department of Education to merge public school education with rigorous dance training. Students from the school, grades 4 through 8, return to the Joyce Theater with a program including last year's popular "Kydzny," danced to a mix of jazz and Balkan folk music, and other boisterous selections. Friday at 7 p.m., Saturday at 2 and 7 p.m., Sunday at 2 p.m., 175 Eighth Avenue, at 19th Street, Chelsea, 212-242-0800, joyce.org.(Siobhan Burke)
Dance and Process (Friday and Saturday) This long-running series at the Kitchen convenes a small cohort of artists for 10 weeks of conversation and sharing, culminating in two public performances. Participating artists this year are Strauss Bourque-LaFrance, Benjamin Kimitch, Courtney Krantz and Elizabeth Ward, working under the guidance of the choreographer Sarah Michelson. At 7:30 p.m., The Kitchen, 512 West 19th Street, Chelsea, 212-255-5793, thekitchen.org. (Schaefer)
Dances for a Variable Population (through Saturday) Naomi Goldberg Haas directs this company of dancers ages 25 to 85, who bring a range of experiences and abilities to her site-specific work. In the new "10027," a cast of West Harlem residents and illustrious guest artists, including Gus Solomons Jr. and Loretta Abbott, roam between three locales in the titular zipcode: a garden, a basketball court and a public-housing project. For audience members who prefer a more stationary experience, excerpts from all three sections will be presented in one place on Saturday. Friday at 6 p.m., starting at Morningside Gardens, 100 La Salle Street, between Broadway and Amsterdam; Saturday at 5 p.m., Old Broadway between 125th and 126th Streets; dvpnyc.org. (Burke)
Hudson River Dance Festival (Wednesday and Thursday) Dance comes to the west side where city meets water with a new, free festival at Pier 63 in Hudson River Park. The inaugural lineup features a trifecta of smart and entertaining dances from three popular companies: The Paul Taylor Dance Company contributes "Company B," set to music by the Andrews Sisters; Parsons Dance brings "Nascimento," an ode to the legendary Brazilian composer of that name; and Ballet Hispanico puts its talented men forward in the sultry "Sombrerísimo." At 6:30 p.m., Hudson River Park between West 22nd and West 24th Streets, Chelsea, 212-627-2020, hudsonriverpark.org. (Schaefer)
* Jacobs Pillow Dance Festival (Saturday and Sunday) In the immensely entertaining production "Three Acts, Two Dancers, One Radio Host," which kicks off this summer-long festival, the choreographer Monica Bill Barnes, the dancer Anna Bass and the radio host Ira Glass combine the two forms in clever and unexpectedly poignant ways. Mr. Glass subjects Ms. Barnes and Ms. Bass to his disarmingly sharp interrogation; they, in turn, make him move. (Festival continues with various programming through Aug. 29.) Saturday at 8 p.m., Sunday at 2 p.m., 358 George Carter Road, Becket, Mass., 413-243-0745, jacobspillow.org. (Schaefer)
* Bill T. Jones/Arnie Zane Dance Company (Thursday through June 21) The first work in a planned trilogy of biographically inspired works takes Bill T. Jones to World War II-era France where Dora Amelan, a French-Jewish nurse and social worker, navigated the horrors of the Holocaust and contributed to the underground resistance. "Analogy/Dora: Tramontane" dramatizes the story of Ms. Amelan, who is now 94, with Mr. Jones's effective blend of narrative and emotionally resonant abstraction. Thursday and next Friday at 7:30 p.m., June 20 at 8 p.m., June 21 at 3 p.m., Alexander Kasser Theater, Montclair State University, 1 Normal Avenue, Montclair, 973-655-5112, peakperfs.org. (Schaefer)
* La MaMa Moves! Dance Festival (through June 21) This East Village festival is entering its 10th year of supporting adventurous dance, with a busy lineup of local and international artists. For the final stretch of programming, Alexandra Beller presents "milkdreams," an attempt to strip away the learned behavior of dance training and generate movement from a joyous source. Thursdays through Saturdays at 8 p.m., Sundays at 4 p.m., 74A East Fourth Street, East Village, 212-475-7710, lamama.org. (Burke)
* Blanca Li (through Sunday) The Hollywood pitch for Blanca Li's "Robot" might be "Ex Machina" meets "Alice in Wonderland." In this multimedia and very colorful production, the Spanish choreographer takes artificial intelligence down the rabbit hole. People act like robots, robots act like people, and things get rather fun and outrageous. Ms. Li trained in flamenco and mixes its splashy drama with her love for fashion and the avant-garde, making an intelligent spectacle in the process. Friday at 8 p.m., Saturday at 2 and 8 p.m., Sunday at 1 and 5 p.m., Howard Gilman Opera House, Brooklyn Academy of Music, 30 Lafayette Avenue, at Ashland Place, Fort Greene, 718-636-4100, bam.org. (Schaefer)
Monte/Buglisi/Muller (Tuesday through June 20) Last year, the veteran choreographers Elisa Monte, Jacqulyn Buglisi and Jennifer Muller presented a shared evening of work, and they've teamed up again. This year's theme is "Reflections on Water." Ms. Muller's contribution is a world premiere called "Alchemy," which she applies to both physical matter and emotional evolution; Ms. Monte offers "Hurricane Deck," with four couples who choose a different ending each night; and Ms. Buglisi meditates on the environment with "Sand" and the new "Sacred Landscapes 5." At 7:30 p.m., New York Live Arts, 219 West 19th Street, Chelsea, 212-924-0077, newyorklivearts.org. (Schaefer)
New Chamber Ballet (Friday and Saturday) Concluding his company's 2014-15 season, the director Miro Magloire introduces new work of his own as well as from the company's resident choreographer, Constantine Baecher. As usual, casual intimacy in a recital-like setting mixes with a welcome range of contemporary music, played live. The results are often fresh and rewarding. This performance features seven dancers, two musicians and six works, including the premiere of Mr. Magloire's "Friction," a duet by Mr. Baecher, and a tribute to the new nonagenarian Pierre Boulez. At 8 p.m., City Center, 131 West 55th Street, Manhattan, 212-868-4444, newchamberballet.com. (Schaefer)
* New York Theater Ballet (Thursday through June 20) The latest iteration of this charming chamber troupe's Legends & Visionaries series is notably Anglophilic. The program includes work by the English masters Frederick Ashton ("Capriol Suite"), Antony Tudor ("Dark Elegies") and Richard Alston ("Such Longing"), as well as a voice from the next generation -- that of Gemma Bond, an English member of American Ballet Theater's corps, who will debut a new work. David Parker, the sole American of the bunch, chips in a world premiere as well. At 8 p.m., Danspace Project, St. Mark's Church, 131 East 10th Street, East Village, 866-811-4111, danspaceproject.org. (Schaefer)
Polish National Ballet (Tuesday through June 21) On its first trip to New York, the Polish National Ballet has decided to leave the classics at home. Its first impression is a trio of contemporary works, with two from Krzysztof Pastor, the company's director since 2009. The third is an interpretation of the oft-told tale of sacrifice, "Rite of Spring," by the France-based Israeli choreographer Emanuel Gat, who seeds his version with Latin American-inspired movement. Tuesday and Wednesday at 7:30 p.m., Thursday and next Friday at 8 p.m., June 20 at 2 and 8 p.m., June 21 at 2 p.m., Joyce Theater, 175 Eighth Avenue, at 19th Street, Chelsea, 212-242-0800, joyce.org. (Schaefer)
Anna Sperber (Friday and Saturday) Last year, Gibney Dance took over the former Dance New Amsterdam space near City Hall. A new studio theater there inspired the Brooklyn-based choreographer Anna Sperber to create "Ruptured Horizon," part of the company's spring season, called "Making Space." Ms. Sperber uses the studio as a blank canvas, which she paints with the dancers Michael Ingle, Rebecca Warner, Omagbitse Omagbemi and Alice MacDonald. Architecture and light are her muses. At 7:30 p.m., Gibney Performing Arts Center, 280 Broadway, near Chambers Street, Lower Manhattan, 646-837-6809, gibneydance.org. (Schaefer)
Wildcat! (through Sunday) This interdisciplinary collective -- made up of the performers Jeremy Toussaint-Baptiste, Eleni Zaharopoulos and André M. Zachery -- presents two programs billed jointly as "Assembly," voicing a call to action around issues of gender, racial and economic inequality. The first, "I Do Mind Dying: Danse Précarité" (Saturday) takes inspiration from the Detroit blues singer Joe L. Carter. The second, "3 Meaningful Meditations" (Friday and Sunday), comprises solo works by each member of the collective. These are the culminating events of Jack's season-long "Forward Ferguson" series. Friday and Saturday at 8 p.m., Sunday at 3 p.m., Jack, 505 1/2 Waverly Avenue, near Fulton Street, Clinton Hill, Brooklyn, jackny.org. (Burke)
Works and Process: Wendy Whelan & Edward Watson (Sunday and Monday) In October, Wendy Whelan retired from the New York City Ballet after 30 years. Last month, she was back on stage as the muse of four contemporary choreographers in the work "Restless Creature." Proving she is restless indeed, Ms. Whelan is now collaborating with Edward Watson, a brazen principal dancer with the Royal Ballet, on a project featuring new work by Annie-B Parson, Arthur Pita and Danièle Desnoyers. Ahead of the premiere, they visit the Guggenheim to perform excerpts and chat about the process. Sunday at 3 and 7:30 p.m., Monday at 7:30 p.m., Peter B. Lewis Theater, 1071 Fifth Avenue, at 89th Street, 212-423-3575, worksandprocess.org. (Some shows sold out.) (Schaefer)
