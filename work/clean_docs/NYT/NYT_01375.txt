After an interim of apparent wedded bliss elsewhere, the participatory Off Broadway play "Tony n' Tina's Wedding," last seen flinging ziti and zingers near Times Square in 2010, will be bringing its raucous participatory spirit back to New York City.
A new production of the show, in which audience members play the friends and family of the bride and groom, will begin performances on March 5, in a version featuring a "ceremony" at the Jacqueline Kennedy Onassis High School on West 46th Street, a procession down Shubert Alley, and a traditional Italian-American wedding banquet at Guy's American Kitchen and Bar, complete with old-school wedding singer. No casting has been announced.
When a movie version of the play, created by the Artificial Intelligence comedy troupe and first performed Off Broadway in 1988, was released in 2007, critics blasted its worn clichés of gum-chewing bridesmaids, pot-smoking groomsmen and bathroom-joke treatment of holy water. But Joe Corcoran, the show's lead producer, said that the rise of social media had injected fresh potential into the participatory concept, while changes in attitudes toward marriage had given the show new material to play with, including a possible "Tony n' Tony's Wedding" variant.
"Nothing is really better than a wedding," said Mr. Corcoran, who, incidentally, met his own wife during a performance. "There's so much drama to play with."
