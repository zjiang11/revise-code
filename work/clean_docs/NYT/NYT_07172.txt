Zachary Daniel Groff and Lucas Daniel Gonzaga de Freitas were married Sept. 14 in Baltimore. Rabbi Binyamin R. Biber officiated at the Baltimore Museum of Art, with Toni Cupal, the couple's yoga teacher and a spiritual counselor, taking part in the ceremony.
On Nov. 29, the couple is to have a second ceremony that will include Jewish and Buddhist marriage traditions, and is to be led by Vânia Weitzel, a nondenominational celebrant, at Villa Rizza, an events space in Brasília, Brazil.
Mr. Groff (left), 27, is a doctoral student in economics at Stanford. He graduated magna cum laude from Yale.
He is the son of Lynne Hochheiser Groff and Alfred C. Groff of Potomac, Md. The groom's mother, a certified public accountant, was a partner in Krawchick & Company, an accounting firm in Larchmont, N.Y., before becoming a stay-at-home parent. His father retired in June from Shearman & Sterling, the New York law firm, where he was of counsel and had been a partner.
Mr. Freitas, 26, is a machine-learning engineer on the YouTube team, in San Bruno, Calif., at Google. He is also a chairman of the One to Change the World Society, a young professionals group that supports Mercy for Animals, a Los Angeles organization that is working to stop animal cruelty on farms. He graduated from Harvard.
He is a son of Vania L. Gonzaga de Freitas and Daniel Freitas of Brasília. The groom's mother retired as the principal at Promoção Educativa do Menor, a public middle school in Brasília. His father is a freelance computer technician in Brasília.
The couple, who will use the surname Freitas-Groff, met in 2016 through their mutual interest in animal-rights advocacy. Mr. Groff was speaking on the issue in a Google hangout, and Mr. Freitas asked a question.
They met at an animal-rights conference in Los Angeles a few weeks later.
