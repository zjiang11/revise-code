BRAINFEST!
If you visit the American Museum of Natural History on Saturday, don't be surprised if someone tosses a brick at you or drops a ruler on your toes. It's all in the name of science.
The bricks, actually soft synthetics, illustrate how the brain uses past experience to interpret sensory information. "We're going to show visitors an actual brick and have them pick it up and hold it," said Bella Desai, the museum's associate director of public programs and exhibition education. Then a staff member may ask them to catch a thrown brick. Although it's a lightweight fake, "people always tense up," Ms. Desai said.
The demonstration is part of "Trick Your Senses," a section in BrainFest!, an afternoon of using -- and sometimes losing -- your head. Held in conjunction with the museum's exhibition "Brain: The Inside Story," BrainFest! will offer activities, games and experiments that expand on the show's themes. (Above, simulated neurons respond to hand movements, showing how these cells communicate.)
In the ruler exercise two people face each other. One drops a ruler, always from the same height but at random intervals, while the other tries to catch it. "It's all about improving your reaction time," Ms. Desai said. Part of a section on cognition, it proves the adage "Practice makes perfect."
The festival will bring together enough guides, educators and scientists to offer many opportunities not found in an ordinary stroll through the exhibition, like watching the dissection of sheep brains and cow eyes (in the Sackler Educational Laboratory) or playing Ping-Pong, a workout that emphasizes hand-eye coordination. "A neuroscientist will explain the parts of your brain that are used when you're playing," Ms. Desai said.
And even better than a Ping-Pong paddle, "you can hold a real human brain," she said. The museum will have several, preserved by plastination, a silicone technique.
The festival will also investigate brain-related technology. Wakamaru, a Mitsubishi robot, will greet visitors. "It's a humanoid robot, and he does talk," Ms. Desai said. Children can also try their hands at manipulating emergency robots, like those used to approach hazardous materials.
But for all the wonders of artificial intelligence, the real marvel here is the human kind. The message of BrainFest!: Gray matter matters.
