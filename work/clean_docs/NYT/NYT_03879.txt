Though crowns have never been part of her professional headgear, Lois Smith is indisputably royalty in the American theater. Ms. Smith, who improbably turned 85 last month, has been one of our most adventurous and prolific actresses since she made her Broadway debut in 1952. Her very presence evokes a host of tantalizing memories of fabled productions, from Tennessee Williams's "Orpheus Descending" (in which she played the fey, phantasmal Carol Cutrere) to the Steppenwolf Theater's "The Grapes of Wrath" (as the indomitable Ma Joad).
So her appearance as a woman reconstructing her past (with the assistance of artificial intelligence) in Jordan Harrison's "Marjorie Prime," which opens on Monday, Dec. 14, at Playwrights Horizons under Anne Kauffman's direction, is guaranteed to be charged with all sorts of mnemonic resonances. Staged in 2014 in an acclaimed production at the Mark Taper Forum in Los Angeles, this searching comedy became a Pulitzer Prize finalist and the basis for a coming film, also starring -- happily -- Ms. Smith.
